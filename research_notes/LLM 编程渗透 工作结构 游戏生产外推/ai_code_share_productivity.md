## 1. Company disclosures: how much code do firms say AI writes, and what exactly is counted?

### Takeaway
Self-reported "share of code written by AI" climbed steeply at the frontier firms. Google went from >25% (Oct 2024) to ~50% (fall 2025) to 75% (Apr 2026). Anthropic went from "70–90%" rhetoric to a measured "80% of code authored by Claude" (Sep 2026). Chinese majors cluster at 40–50%+ (Baidu >43%, Tencent >50%). Almost none of these figures come with a definition. Where a definition exists, it usually means AI-suggested characters or lines that an engineer accepted. That is not the same as "work done by AI", and the figures are not comparable across firms.

### Inferences
- The headline disclosures follow a clear S-curve at the frontier. Google went from ~25% to 50% to 75% in about 18 months (Oct 2024 → Apr 2026), roughly +25 percentage points every 6–9 months. Anthropic reached about 80% measured by Sep 2026. Chinese majors sat about 12 months behind, at 40–50% in 2025–early 2026.
- Firms have changed *which metric* they disclose, not just its level. The 2023–2024 metrics were accepted autocomplete characters. The 2025–2026 metrics shift toward "agent-assisted diffs" (Meta), "authored by Claude" (Anthropic) and "code shipped" (Anthropic 8×). The later figures therefore measure a more autonomous kind of contribution, but they are even less comparable over time.
- Almost every disclosure is made by a firm that sells AI coding tools (Google, Microsoft, Anthropic, OpenAI, Amazon, Baidu, Tencent) or that is signalling "AI-first" to investors (Meta, Salesforce, Robinhood, Coinbase, Spotify). None is audited. Treat them as upper-bound marketing figures unless a definition and a denominator are given.

### Gaps
- No Microsoft update after Apr 2025 turned up. A claim that "Microsoft expects 60% by 2026" appears only in a low-quality Medium post and has no primary source. Kevin Scott's widely cited "95% of code AI-generated by 2030" forecast could not be re-sourced this session.
- I could not retrieve 2025–26 code-share numbers for Amazon, Alibaba (通义灵码/Qoder; only "一人抵三人" narratives surfaced) or Ant Group (CodeFuse). ByteDance has usage numbers only.
- Primary URLs for the Salesforce, Robinhood, Coinbase, Spotify, Shopify and Duolingo statements could not be re-fetched because the search budget was exhausted. The figures above are [P] and need checking.
- Google has never published the definition behind the 25/50/75% series, and it has not reconciled the series with its Jun 2024 "50% of characters" metric.

## 2. Independent measurement: what do outside data say about AI's share of code, commits and PRs?

### Takeaway
Independent measures corroborate the *direction* and rough *order of magnitude* of the 2024 claims. About 30% of US-authored public Python functions were AI-written by the end of 2024, consistent with Google's ">25%". Independent data cannot yet verify the 2026 claims of 75–100%. The best 2026 outside signals come from three sources:
- SemiAnalysis: about 4% of all public GitHub commits carried Claude Code attribution in Feb 2026, projected at 20%+ by end-2026.
- My own GitHub counts: public PR creation per day was flat in 2023–24, then rose 4.7× between Sep 2024 and Sep 2026. Agent bots that open PRs under their own identity account for only about 0.3–3% of PRs; most agent output is filed under human accounts and cannot be attributed.

### Cited Findings
- **Daniotti, Wachs, Feng & Neffke, "Who is using AI to code? Global diffusion and impact of generative AI"** (arXiv, Jun 2025). [P] — [arXiv 2506.08945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08945)
  - Method: they trained a classifier to detect AI-generated Python functions and applied it to tens of millions of GitHub commits (≈80M, as recalled).
  - Result: AI-written code was an estimated 30.1% of Python functions from US contributors by the end of 2024. Other countries: Germany 24.3%, France 23.2%, India 21.6%, Russia 15.4%, China 11.7%.
  - Newer GitHub users use AI more than veterans.
  - Within-developer models: moving to 30% AI use is associated with about 2.4% more quarterly commits, plus more exploration of new libraries.
  - They estimate the annual US value of AI-assisted coding at $9.6–14.4B, or $64–96B if the larger RCT effect sizes are assumed.
  - These numbers are from the v1 abstract as recalled. Later versions or a journal version may differ.
- **SemiAnalysis, "Claude Code is the Inflection Point"** (early Feb 2026). [S] — [SemiAnalysis newsletter](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/claude-code-is-the-inflection-point); [SemiAnalysis on X](https://x.com/SemiAnalysis_/status/2027443723362042308?lang=en); [GIGAZINE, 10 Feb 2026](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260210-claude-code-github-commits-4-percent-20-percent/); [OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/4-of-github-commits-are-now-made-by-claude-code-semianalysis-report/)

  - Projection: "Claude Code will be 20%+ of all daily commits by the end of 2026."
  - Inflection points were in Oct 2025 and Jan 2026.
  - Method as reported: it counts commits carrying Claude Code attribution, such as co-author trailers. This undercounts users who strip the trailer and overcounts "work", because agent commits tend to be smaller and more frequent.
- A third-party tracker titled "The Exponential Rise of Claude Code to 326K+ Daily Commits" suggests the daily count kept climbing after February. Its date and method are unknown. [S] — [CoreMention](https://coremention.com/blog/claude-code-tracker/)

  - Adopting Cursor produced a large but *transient* rise in project velocity (lines added and commits), which faded after about 1–2 months.
  - It also produced a *persistent* rise in static-analysis warnings and code complexity, which in turn predicts later slowdowns. Exact magnitudes were not re-verified.

### Inferences
**Claims versus measurements**
- In 2024, claims and measurements agree. Google's ">25%" in Oct 2024 and Daniotti's 30% of US Python functions at end-2024 describe the same autocomplete-era phenomenon: AI-drafted, human-accepted code.
- In 2026, claims run far ahead of what outside data can check. The 75–100% figures refer to firms' *internal* repos under heavy dogfooding.
- The only outside share measure for 2026, SemiAnalysis's 4% of public commits from a single agent, is a different quantity. It captures fully agent-attributed commits across all of GitHub, including hobbyists and non-adopters, and it misses autocomplete-assisted human commits.

**What the PR-volume curve shows (my inference)**
- Human developer growth runs at about 20–25% a year (Octoverse). A 4.7× rise in daily public PR creation over two years, after a flat 2023–24, is far more than that growth can explain. The step-ups line up with the launches of CLI and cloud agents: Claude Code GA in May 2025, Codex in May 2025, the Copilot agent in May 2025, Sonnet 4.5 in Sep 2025, and Opus 4.5 in Nov 2025.
- This implies that a large share of *new* PR volume in 2026 is agent-mediated, even though most of it is filed under human accounts.
- The volume is output, not value. The AIDev finding of lower acceptance and the Cursor quality findings argue against equating PR counts with work done.

- I found no independent measurement of AI share in *private or enterprise* codebases. Faros, Jellyfish, LinearB and similar telemetry vendors hold such data, but I retrieved no 2026 figures from them.

## 3. Rigorous productivity evidence: RCTs, field experiments and quasi-experiments, including null and negative findings

### Takeaway
Evidence from controlled studies is mixed and depends on the task.
- Well-scoped or greenfield tasks, especially for less experienced developers, show gains of +20% to +56% (Peng 2023, Google 2024, Cui et al. 2024/25).
- Experienced developers working in their own large, mature codebases were 19% *slower* in early 2025 (METR). METR's Aug 2025 follow-up hints at a speedup, but selection bias made it unreliable.
- Telemetry and survey studies find more individual output (+21% tasks, +98% PRs) but no organisation-level gains in delivery or stability, plus persistent quality costs (DORA 2024, Uplevel, Faros, CMU/Cursor).
- The newest vendor-internal numbers (Anthropic's 8× code shipped) are much larger but are neither controlled nor audited.

### Inferences
- Effect size falls as realism rises. Toy and greenfield tasks show +56%, enterprise single tasks +21%, and field output counts +26%. Expert, mature-codebase work showed −19% in early 2025, and org-level delivery shows about 0% or worse (DORA 2024, Faros).
- The "perception gap" is itself a finding. In METR's study, developers believed they were 20% faster while measured as 19% slower. Self-reported gains (Anthropic's +50%, DORA's >80% perceiving gains) should therefore be discounted.
- The bottleneck moves downstream. Faros found review time +91%, Song et al. integration time +41.6%, and Anthropic 25× CI load with "writing code is no longer the constraint". AI accelerates code production faster than organisations can absorb, verify and ship it.
- The evidence base lags the tools by 6–12 months. Every rigorous study used tools from before the Claude 4 generation (pre mid-2025). There is no rigorous evidence yet for the agentic generation of late 2025–2026. METR's attempt was undermined because developers now refuse to work without AI.

### Gaps
- There is no published RCT or quasi-experiment on agentic tools (Claude Code, Codex, Copilot agent) from 2026. METR's redesigned study had not reported by Sep 2026, as far as I could find.
- I found no 2026 DORA or Faros update this session. DORA 2026 is typically released in September or October, so it may be imminent or just out.
- Exact confidence intervals and sub-sample sizes for Cui et al. and the Google RCT were not re-verified.

## 4. Automation versus augmentation: the autonomy ladder over time

### Takeaway
Usage has moved up an autonomy ladder:
- inline completion (2021–23)
- chat (2023)
- agentic IDE modes (late 2024)
- terminal and cloud agents (2025)
- background, unattended and multi-agent work (late 2025–2026)

Anthropic's usage telemetry shows automation growing in phases rather than steadily:
- Directive automation rose from 27% to 39% of Claude.ai conversations between late 2024 and Aug 2025, when automation first exceeded augmentation (49% vs 47%).
- On Claude.ai, augmentation then regained the lead (52% vs 45% in Nov 2025), because coding migrated off Claude.ai into Claude Code and the API.
- Claude Code itself is heavily automation-oriented (79% vs 49% on Claude.ai in Apr 2025), and its sessions keep getting longer and more autonomous.

## 5. Share of developer working time that is AI-assisted

### Takeaway
By late 2025 AI was near-universal in developer work. About 90% of developers use it, with a median of about 2 hours a day (DORA 2025). At the frontier, AI touched most of the working day by late 2025: Anthropic engineers used Claude in 59% of their work, up from 28% a year earlier. Frontier engineers said in 2026 that they no longer hand-write code at all. Even so, in late 2025 more than half of Anthropic engineers could "fully delegate" only 0–20% of their work. AI touches most work, but humans still own most tasks end to end.

## 6. Capability trajectory, and how it lines up with adoption

### Takeaway
Agentic coding capability rose by more than an order of magnitude on every tracked metric:
- SWE-bench: ~2% on the original benchmark (Oct 2023), then 33% on SWE-bench Verified (Aug 2024), 49% (Oct 2024), ~72–75% (mid-2025), ~81% (late 2025) and 93.9% (Apr 2026). The benchmark is now saturated and contaminated, and labs report newer suites instead (SWE-bench Pro, Terminal-Bench 2.0→4.0, FrontierCode, CursorBench).
- METR's 50% time horizon went from about 1 hour (Claude 3.7 Sonnet, early 2025) to at least 16 hours (Claude Mythos Preview, spring 2026). The doubling time shortened from about 7 months (2019–25) to about 4.3 months (post-2023) and about 3 months (post-2024).
- Frontier per-token prices fell 3–7× for top-tier models between 2023 and 2026, but tokens per task rose sharply.

Adoption step-changes follow major model releases within about 1–3 months.

### Inferences
- **The curve's shape.** The frontier AI "share of code" rose roughly logistically: ~25% (late 2024) → ~50% (late 2025) → 75–80% (mid/late 2026) at Google and Anthropic. The *broader ecosystem* lags. In public repos, one agent produced ~4% of commits in early 2026, and China's typical firm was at 20–30% in H2 2025. Capability doubling every ~3–4 months (METR) is the underlying driver. Adoption steps follow model releases with about a 1–3 month lag and are amplified by product form factor (CLI and background agents in 2025, multi-agent in 2026).
- **Capability overhang.** The METR 50% horizon (≥16 human-hours in spring 2026) far exceeds typical deployed autonomous run lengths (p99.9 of 45 wall-clock minutes in Jan 2026; the median turn is 45 s). The two metrics differ in definition, but the gap suggests that deployment, verification and trust, not raw capability, bound AI's share of workload. That fits the bottleneck-shift evidence: CI load 25×, review time +91%.
- **Benchmark saturation versus real work.** SWE-bench Verified went from 2% to 94% in about 30 months while RCT productivity effects ranged from −19% to +56%. Benchmark gains overstate real-world workload substitution. SWE-bench Verified has been superseded by Pro, Terminal-Bench 4.0 and FrontierCode, where Sep 2026 frontier scores sit around 55–66%, leaving headroom for further gains in delegable work.
- **Cost.** Per-token prices for the top tier fell about 3–4× from Opus 4 to Opus 5.5 in about 16 months, far less than Epoch's fixed-capability decline, because each new frontier tier resets the price. Agentic sessions consume many times more tokens, so cost per completed task has probably not fallen as fast as token prices. This is an inference, and no direct data were retrieved.
