# Generative AI in the creative content industries next to game development (2022 → Sept 2026): evidence to bridge from AI-in-coding to game production

*How to read these notes. Every finding carries an evidence tag: **[Measured]** means platform, administrative or transaction data, or an econometric study. **[Survey]** is self-report. **[Anecdote]** is an individual case or press account. **[Prediction]** is a forecast. **[Vendor/PR]** is a claim from a company selling the technology. **[Policy/Contract]** covers platform rules, union contracts and deals. ## 1. Online freelance labor-market evidence (writing, image and translation work) and the translation market

### Takeaway
The best causal evidence from Upwork-type platforms (2022–2025) shows that AI-substitutable text work lost demand first and hardest. Writing and translation job posts fell about 20–50% against counterfactuals, and earnings fell too. Even top freelancers were hit. Image jobs declined after Midjourney, DALL-E and Stable Diffusion. Total freelance demand did not fall, though. It moved toward complementary skills and toward remaining jobs that were more complex and better paid, while novices lost out. Translation is the best-documented casualty. Machine translation was already cutting U.S. translator employment growth before LLMs, and in China more than two-thirds of translation projects are now machine translation or machine translation plus post-editing.

### Cited Findings
**Freelance-platform studies**
- [Measured] Hui, Reshef & Zhou (Organization Science 2024; SSRN 2023) studied an online labor platform (Upwork) around the releases of ChatGPT, DALL-E 2 and Midjourney. After ChatGPT, writing-related freelancers saw monthly jobs fall 2% and monthly earnings fall 5.2%. Image-related freelancers saw similar effects after the image generators launched. Top freelancers were disproportionately affected. The authors conclude that in the short term gen AI reduces demand for knowledge workers of all types. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4527336); [WashU Olin news](https://olin.washu.edu/about/news-and-media/news/2023/08/study-ai-tools-cause-a-decline-in-freelance-work-and-incomeat-least-in-the-short-run.php); [CEPR VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/artificial-intelligence-and-its-short-term-effects-employment); [Edward Conard summary](https://www.edwardconard.com/macro-roundup/xianghui90-oren_reshef-and-zhou_yu_ai-find-that-the-release-of-chatgpt-led-to-a-2-drop-in-the-number-of-jobs-of-freelancers-on-the-upwork-platform-and-a-5-2-drop-in-their-monthly-earnings/?view=detail)
- [Measured] Demirci, Hannane & Zhu (Management Science 2025) found a 21% drop in job posts for automation-prone writing and coding jobs, relative to manual-intensive jobs, within eight months of ChatGPT. Image-related job posts also fell significantly after AI image generators appeared. With fewer posts, competition among freelancers rose, and the automation-prone jobs that remained were more complex and paid more. — [Management Science](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05420); [Harvard D^3](https://d3.harvard.edu/who-is-ai-replacing/); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4602944)
- [Measured] Teutloff et al. (J. Economic Behavior & Organization vol. 235, 2025) split postings on a leading freelancing platform into 116 skill clusters labelled substitutable, complementary or unaffected. Demand for substitutable skills such as writing and translation fell 20–50% relative to the counterfactual, and by up to 50% in short-term roles. Demand rose only in complementary or unaffected clusters. Inside complementary roles, demand for novices fell. Total freelance demand did not decrease. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268124004591); [Oxford Internet Institute](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/news-events/the-winners-and-losers-of-generative-ai-in-the-freelance-job-market/); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1072038)
- [Measured; industry analysis, not peer-reviewed] Bloomberry analyzed about 5M Upwork posts from 1 Nov 2022 to 14 Feb 2024 using an 84-day moving average. Writing jobs fell 33%, translation 19% and customer service 16%. Other categories rose: video editing/production +39%, web design +10%, graphic design +8%, backend development +6%, frontend development +4%. — [Bloomberry](https://bloomberry.com/blog/i-analyzed-5m-freelancing-jobs-to-see-what-jobs-are-being-replaced-by-ai/); [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/ai-is-taking-over-these-freelancing-jobs-the-most-report/471183)
- [Conceptual/argument paper; the empirical content could not be verified] Rajpal, "From Producing to Validating: How AI Is Deskilling Freelancers" (arXiv 2608.26089, submitted 26 Aug 2026), names a "producing-to-validating shift": clients stop commissioning original work and start commissioning repairs of AI output. The paper follows this through two cases, machine-translation post-editing and software development. It argues that discount pricing for correction work persists even when correcting takes more expertise than producing the original. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26089)

**Translation market**
- [Measured] Frey & Llanos-Paredes (Oxford Martin School 2025) studied 695 U.S. local labor markets after 2010. Each 1 pp rise in Google Translate use was associated with a 0.71 pp drop in translator employment growth. Cumulatively that is about 28,000 translator positions not created between 2010 and 2023. Machine translation also reduced demand for foreign-language skills in Spanish, Chinese, Japanese, French and German. — [Oxford Martin](https://www.oxfordmartin.ox.ac.uk/publications/lost-in-translation-artificial-intelligence-and-the-demand-for-foreign-language-skills); [paper PDF](https://oms-www.files.svdcdn.com/production/downloads/academic/Frey_LlanosParedes_2025_LostInTranslation.pdf)
- [Survey] The UK Society of Authors surveyed about 12,500 members and other authors in January 2024 and received about 800 responses. Among translators, 36% had already lost work to gen AI and 43% said their income had fallen because of it. More than three-quarters expected a negative effect on future income. 37% had used gen AI in their work, and 8% did so because a publisher or commissioner asked. About a quarter of illustrators had lost work. — [European Writers' Council](https://europeanwriterscouncil.eu/soa-survey-uk-ai-2024/); [Lit Hub](https://lithub.com/more-than-a-third-of-translators-think-theyve-already-lost-work-to-ai/); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/a-third-of-translators-a-quarter-of-illustrators-have-lost-their-jobs-to-ai); [International Authors Forum](https://internationalauthors.org/news/society-of-authors-ai-survey-results-published/)
- [Company action] Duolingo cut about 10% of its contractor workforce at the end of 2023 as it moved content production and translation onto GPT-4-class models. Those affected were mainly translators, writers and language specialists. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/01/09/duolingo-cut-10-of-its-contractor-workforce-as-the-company-embraces-ai); [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2024/01/10/duolingo-ai-layoffs/)
- [Company policy] In 2025 Duolingo's CEO published an "AI-first" memo. It said the company would gradually stop using contractors for work AI can do and would hire only when a team could automate no further. Users and linguists pushed back, pointing to quality problems such as missed dialect and formality distinctions. The CEO later said full-time staff were not being replaced. — [Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2025/ai/duolingo-goes-ai-first-as-it-phases-out-human-contractors); [HR Grapevine](https://www.hrgrapevine.com/us/content/article/2025-08-19-no-layoffs-for-full-time-staff-duolingo-ceo-clarifies-ai-plans-after-memo-controversy)
- [Anecdote] CNN (23 Jan 2026) reported that translators are asked to polish machine output that then trains their replacements ("dig your own professional grave"). One translator lost about 70% of his income when EU translation work dried up. — [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/01/23/tech/translation-language-jobs-ai-automation-intl)
- [Measured/association report, China] Covered in §7: according to the Translators Association of China (TAC), more than 66% of 2025 projects were done by machine translation or machine translation with human post-editing, and industry output fell 1.0%.

### Inferences
- The order in freelance markets was text first and biggest (writing and translation), then images. Complementary work such as video editing and AI integration grew. The work affected first is standardized, text-shaped and cheap to check.
- The shock falls on the mix of work, not on its total. Novice and commodity tasks disappear. Complex and specialist work survives and is paid more. Early AI-in-coding evidence points the same way, with entry-level roles displaced.
- For game production, the most exposed layer is the one that looks like Upwork: outsourced localization text, marketing copy, and generic 2D asset commissions. Senior and specialist roles are complemented rather than replaced.

### Gaps
- Exact magnitudes for image-related jobs in Hui et al. and Demirci et al. could not be verified here. A commonly cited figure of about −17% in image-related posts in Demirci et al. was not confirmed in this session.
- A claim that Upwork translation hourly wages fell more than 20% appeared in search summaries but could not be traced to a primary source.
- No peer-reviewed platform-level update covering 2025–2026 was found, so it is unknown whether the effects deepened with the 2025 image and video models. Liu et al., "'Generate' the Future of Work" (arXiv 2308.05201), was not reviewed.
- No copywriting labor data outside freelance platforms was found (BLS writer employment, agency headcount).
- The Rajpal paper's full text and any quantitative post-editing rate data could not be read because arXiv and the Pith mirror were blocked.

## 2. Illustration and concept art, and mass production of ad creatives

### Takeaway
Commercial 2D illustration, especially outsourced game art in China, was the first creative craft with a visible labor shock, in H1 2023. Commissions for promo posters and character art dried up or turned into "fix the AI image" jobs at a tenth to a quarter of former rates. Western surveys through 2025 show smaller losses: about a quarter of UK illustrators and about a fifth of Dutch freelance artists reported lost work or income. In advertising, ad platforms are taking creative production in-house. Meta reported more than 8M advertisers using its gen-AI creative tools by Q1 2026 and aims to fully automate ad creation by the end of 2026. TikTok's Symphony generates complete videos with scripts, voiceovers and avatars. Performance-ad variants are the most automated visual output because A/B metrics check quality automatically. Brand and "hero" ads still draw audience backlash.

### Inferences
- The illustration shock hit where art is a commodity input that the buyer can judge as "good enough" at a glance: promo posters, character splash drafts, marketing art. It also hit where the buyer can run the tool itself, which describes game companies commissioning outsourcers. It hit least where style consistency across many assets, IP clearance, or a client-imposed no-AI rule mattered.
- The price mechanism mirrors machine-translation post-editing. Commissions become fix or paintover jobs at about 10–25% of the old rate, so output per worker rises while the unit price collapses.
- Ad creative is the visual counterpart of commodity copywriting. Quality is checked automatically by click-through and return-on-ad-spend, creatives have short lives, and platforms bundle the tools. Mobile-game user-acquisition creatives are an ad category, so this is likely the most automated visual output in game businesses, well ahead of in-game art.
- Western illustrators were hit less than Chinese outsourcing illustrators. Plausible reasons are client bans and IP worries, audience backlash, and slower cost pressure. This is an inference; no study decomposes these causes.

### Gaps
- No systematic rate time series for illustration or concept art was found, globally or for China.
- The number of creative variants per campaign before and after gen AI was not found. Meta's widely cited Q3 2024 disclosure ("15 million ads created in a month" by more than 1M advertisers) could not be verified in this session.
- There is no measured share of Chinese or global game art produced with AI.
- A ResetEra thread title says artists were later "being rehired" after the Rest of World story. This is unverified.
- The claim that Meta's advertiser count doubled "in roughly four months" (PPC Land summary) conflicts with the end-2024 to Q1 2026 dates and should not be used.

## 3. Voice acting and dubbing

### Takeaway
Adoption of AI voice has split in two. It spread fast in low-stakes, high-volume narration: corporate and e-learning, ads, China's short-drama dubbing and otome games, and incidental lines in live-service games. It was gated in premium performance by union contracts (the SAG-AFTRA 2025 Interactive Media Agreement after an 11-month strike; Equity's 99.6% ballot), by audience backlash (Amazon pulled its AI anime dubs within days), and by an audible quality gap (Arc Raiders re-recorded some AI lines). The share of voice actors in a NAVA survey who reported losing work directly to AI rose from 14% to 21% in a year. ElevenLabs' revenue shows mass adoption of synthetic voice, much of it in enterprise voice agents rather than dubbing.

### Cited Findings
- [Policy/Contract] SAG-AFTRA's 2025 Interactive Media (video game) Agreement was ratified by 95.04% of members in July 2025, ending an 11-month strike. It requires informed consent and disclosure for AI digital-replica uses, lets performers suspend consent for generating new material during a strike, and sets minimum rates for replica use. — [SAG-AFTRA](https://www.sagaftra.org/sag-aftra-members-approve-2025-video-game-agreement); [SAG-AFTRA agreement page](https://www.sagaftra.org/contracts-industry-resources/interactive/2025-interactive-media-video-game-agreement); [AI Trace summary](https://www.aitrace.org/company/sag-aftra/practice/1f131e05-3c62-495a-9596-4aa1930e8db2)
- [Policy/Contract] Under the agreement, producers may build "Independently Created Digital Replicas" from non-IMA material or gen-AI tools prompted with the performer's name, with freely bargained compensation. Pay rises 15.17% compounded on ratification, then 3% each November for three years. — [Davis+Gilbert](https://www.dglaw.com/sag-aftras-new-video-game-agreement/); [Frankfurt Kurnit Klein & Selz](https://technologylaw.fkks.com/post/102mewu/inside-the-new-sag-aftra-interactive-media-agreement-new-standards-for-ai-and-di)
- [Policy/Contract, as reported] "Real-time generation" uses such as in-game chatbots reportedly pay at least 7.5× minimum scale. — [syndicated trade report](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/25835656-sag-aftra-video-game-deal-includes-ai-consent-guardrails-minimum-rates?tab=book)
- [Policy/Contract] Earlier, in January 2024, SAG-AFTRA signed an AI voice agreement with Replica Studios for licensed digital voice replicas in games. — [SAG-AFTRA](https://www.sagaftra.org/sag-aftra-and-replica-studios-introduce-groundbreaking-ai-voice-agreement-ces)
- [Union action] On 18 December 2025, UK Equity members voted 99.6% "yes" in an indicative ballot, on turnout above 75%, to refuse digital scanning on set until adequate AI protections are secured. In February 2025 Equity had demanded enforcement of performers' data rights against unlawful AI training and replica use. — [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2025/12/equity-british-actors-vote-digital-scand-ai-dispute-1236652329/); [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/film/global/british-actors-industrial-action-ai-1236611771/); [FIA](https://fia-actors.com/2025/02/17/enforce-performers-data-rights-in-ai-equity-tells-entertainment-bosses/)

- [Press, China] NetEase's 《时空中的绘旅人》 and miHoYo's 《未定事件簿》 have used in-house AI to produce character voices. ### Inferences
- Across these sources the pattern in games is consistent. AI covers placeholder or scratch VO, incidental barks and fast live-service updates, from licensed replicas. Humans keep hero and emotional performances. The 2025 IMA turns the "licensed replica" into a priced, consent-based product rather than banning it. That points to hybrid casting, not wholesale replacement, in unionized Western AAA.
- Where no union contract or audience-facing brand risk exists (Chinese short dramas, otome games, e-learning), substitution is faster and reaches mid-tier performers first.
- For voice the binding constraint is audience-perceived quality in emotional delivery. Unlike translation, it cannot be checked cheaply against a reference.

### Gaps

### Inferences
- In moving images, how far AI gets depends on tolerance for artifacts multiplied by the need for consistency. Disposable, low-budget vertical dramas have a low bar and went majority-AI in China within about 18 months. Premium series and features have a high bar for shot and character continuity, resolution and art direction, and still use AI shot by shot.
- Studio models trained only on proprietary data hit a data-scale wall, as Lionsgate did. General models trained on scraped data hit a rights wall. Licensing deals without training rights, like Disney–OpenAI, are the compromise that emerged. Game publishers with large asset libraries face the same trade-off.
- For 3D the gate is pipeline integration more than image quality. Real-time assets need clean topology, UVs, LODs, rigs, polygon budgets and a consistent art direction, and verifying these takes technical-art review. That explains why executives expected 3D modelers to be affected (33%) yet no measured displacement data exists. Expect use first in prototypes, background props and UGC, and last in hero characters.

- No measured data after 2024 on VFX or animation employment attributable to AI; the 2023–2025 U.S. production slump confounds any such analysis.
- The AI provisions of TAG's 2024 contract and of the 2023 SAG-AFTRA and WGA TV/theatrical deals were not verified here.
- Not researched: Japanese anime production use of AI, OpenAI-backed *Critterz*, Amazon's *House of David*, and Chinese animation studios outside 漫剧.
- No share-of-output or labor data for 3D modeling was found. Evidence is limited to executive forecasts and vendor claims.

## 5. Content volume, platform responses and audience acceptance (music, stock, books, video, games)

### Takeaway
Wherever uploading is cheap and open (music streaming services, stock libraries, self-publishing, user video, Steam), the share of AI-generated supply rose within one to three years to between about a tenth and more than half. On Deezer it went from about 10,000 tracks a day in January 2025 to more than 50% of daily uploads by June 2026. On Adobe Stock it went from 2.5% of images in May 2023 to about 48% in April 2025. The share of consumption stayed small: fully AI tracks drew 1–3% of Deezer streams, and 85% of those streams were fraudulent. Platforms responded with labelling, exclusion from recommendations, demonetization, upload throttles and spam purges. Rights holders turned lawsuits into licensing (WMG–Suno, UMG–Udio, Disney–OpenAI). Audiences largely cannot detect AI, with 97% failing in Deezer/Ipsos' listening test, but they want it labelled (80%) and punish disclosed AI in brand and emotionally valued content.

### Inferences
- The main volume pattern is that AI's share of supply is far above its share of demand. - Because people cannot tell AI output apart but still demand labels, the acceptance gate is about disclosure and authenticity, not detectability. Once labelled, AI content is discounted by a sizeable minority (40–45% would skip or filter AI music). Steam's disclosure rule makes the same mechanism relevant to games.
- The rights gate is becoming a licensing gate, not a ban: WMG–Suno, UMG–Udio and Disney–OpenAI. Stock companies' data-licensing revenue is already volatile (Shutterstock −47% in Q1 2026). A one-off "sell your library for training" income stream is not a stable replacement for lost licensing.

## 6. How the task mix shifted: from making to directing, curating and validating

### Takeaway
Surveys and market evidence agree. Creative workers' AI use starts with editing and enhancement, variant generation and ideation, and human work moves toward selecting and filtering, correcting and paintover, and direction. Designers trust AI output far less than developers do: only 32% of designers can rely on it, while 68% of developers say it improves quality. The validator role is also systematically priced at a discount, as with machine-translation post-editing and "small fixes" at a tenth of the old rate. Demand polarizes. Novices and juniors lose ground, while specialists and new AI-pipeline roles (AIGC technical art, "gacha-pulling" curation) gain. No representative time-use study of creative workers was found.

### Cited Findings
- [Survey; vendor-run] Adobe's first Creators' Toolkit Report (October 2025) surveyed more than 16,000 creators in the U.S., UK, France, Germany, South Korea, Japan, India and Australia. 86% use creative gen AI. The top uses are editing, upscaling and enhancement (55%), generating new images or video (52%), and ideation and brainstorming (48%). 76% say it helped grow their business or brand, and 85% would use an AI agent that learns their creative style. — [Adobe report PDF](https://news.adobe.com/news/downloads/pdfs/2025/10/10282025-inauguraladobecreatorstoolkitreport.pdf); [Adobe News](https://news.adobe.com/news/2025/10/adobe-max-2025-creators-survey); [Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/tech/software/the-ais-have-it-adobe-report-reveals-86-percent-of-creators-use-creative-generative-ai)
- [Survey; vendor-run] Figma's 2025 AI report surveyed 2,500 designers and developers in seven countries in January 2025. 78% of designers say AI makes them more efficient, but only 47% say it makes them better at their role, and only 32% say they can rely on its output. 40% of designers say AI improves the quality of their work, against 68% of developers. 82% of developers are satisfied with AI output. 85% of both groups say learning AI is essential. The share of teams shipping AI products rose from 22% to 34%. — [Figma blog](https://www.figma.com/blog/figma-2025-ai-report-perspectives/); [Roger Wong summary](https://rogerwong.me/2025/04/figmas-2025-ai-report-perspectives-from-designers-and-developers)
- [Measured] On an art platform, text-to-image AI raised output by about 25% and value by about 50%, while average novelty fell. The authors argue that human value moves to ideation and filtering ("generative synesthesia"). — [PNAS Nexus (Zhou & Lee 2024)](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article/3/3/pgae052/7618478)
- [Measured] After ChatGPT, the automation-prone freelance jobs that remained were more complex and paid more. — [Management Science (Demirci et al.)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05420). More than 60% of firms raised full-time translators' pay. — [TAC 2026 report](https://www.tac-online.org.cn/2026-04/28/content_43415316.html); [Marshub summary](https://www.marshub.cn/blog/%E7%BF%BB%E8%AF%91%E8%A1%8C%E4%B8%9A%E8%BF%8E%E6%99%BA%E5%8F%98%EF%BC%9F%E3%80%8A2026%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E7%BF%BB%E8%AF%91%E8%A1%8C%E4%B8%9A%E5%8F%91%E5%B1%95%E6%8A%A5%E5%91%8A/)

### Inferences
- Coding and content share a common structure. Execution (drafting, rendering, translating) moves to the model, and human time moves to specification and prompting, selection, validation and repair, and final "hero" polish. Two things differ. Creative validation has no compiler or unit tests; it depends on an art director's eye, bilingual review or live A/B metrics. Designers also trust AI output much less than developers (Figma 32% vs 68%). So in content the direction/curation layer probably stays larger and more senior than in coding, and depth of penetration will be greatest where market metrics do the validating (ads, user-acquisition creatives).
- The validation layer is paid less even when it needs expertise (MTPE, fix and paintover jobs). For game outsourcing, expect "AI draft + paintover" rate cards to replace per-asset rates, putting pressure on vendor margins and junior artists.
- The first to lose out are novices and outsourced executors, which is consistent across freelance markets, Chinese translation, Chinese game art and CVL's entry-level finding. That breaks the traditional apprenticeship path from junior executor to senior director, the same issue raised for junior developers.

### Gaps

## 8. Synthesis: which creative sub-disciplines AI has penetrated furthest, and the conditions that explain the order

### Takeaway
Order of penetration as of September 2026, deepest first:
1. Text translation and localization, and commodity copywriting.
2. Performance-ad creative variants, stock imagery, e-commerce product visuals, open-upload music (supply side), and China's vertical AI short dramas and comic dramas.
3. Commercial 2D illustration and concept art (outsourced game and marketing art above all), and low-stakes voice (narration, Chinese dubbing, incidental game lines).
4. Premium voice performance, premium film and TV VFX and animation, production-grade 3D modeling, and character animation.

The order is best explained by:
- how cheaply output quality can be checked;
- tolerance for "good enough" commodity output;
- consistency and controllability requirements;
- how easily output slots into existing pipelines;
- the IP and consent regime;
- union contracts;
- audience acceptance, enforced through platform policy;
- local cost pressure.

**Why the order looks like this: a verifiability-and-integration model that can be carried over from coding**
1. **Cheap, reference-based verification comes first.** Translation (compare against the source) and performance ads (the metric decides) behave like code with tests. That is why they went furthest. Illustration is judged at a glance but subjectively, so it went second-fastest in commodity uses. 3D, animation and premium voice need costly expert or in-engine validation, and they lag.
2. **Tolerance for commodity output.** Wherever the buyer accepts "good enough" (UA ads, stock, disposable vertical drama, e-commerce), AI took the majority of new supply. Wherever the content itself is the product and carries emotional or brand value (hero ads, anime dubs, premium series), backlash and quality gaps slowed it.
3. **Consistency over many assets is harder than one-off quality.** Single images automated first. Series of characters, temporal video consistency and 3D topology came later. Games are consistency-heavy (a character must be identical across hundreds of assets and animations), which argues for slower in-game penetration than in marketing.
4. **Pipeline integration.** Text slots into CAT/TMS and images are files, but game-ready 3D and animation must satisfy engine constraints. This parallels coding, where AI penetrated fastest inside existing IDE, test and CI loops.
5. **Institutions set the speed within each tier.** Unions (SAG-AFTRA, Equity) turn outright replacement into consent-based, paid replica licensing, which slows voice in the West. China's lack of such institutions plus cost pressure produced the fastest substitution (illustration in 2023, short dramas in 2026).
6. **Platforms cap the returns to pure volume.** Labelling, recommendation exclusion and demonetization (Deezer, Spotify, YouTube, KDP) mean mass production raises supply much more than it raises consumption or revenue. Steam's disclosure regime puts games on the same path.

**Carrying the model over to game production (explicitly an inference)**
- Expect game content to be automated in this order: localization text → UA and marketing creatives → concept exploration, 2D promo art and UI icons → placeholder and incidental VO → background props and environment set-dressing in 3D → hero art, character animation, cinematics and lead performances.
- Expect the coding pattern to repeat. Output per worker rises. Junior and outsourced execution roles shrink first (evidence: Chinese translation, Chinese game art, CVL entry-level, Teutloff novices). Remaining work moves to direction, curation and validation, which is often priced at a discount (MTPE, paintover). New hybrid pipeline roles appear (AIGC technical artists, "抽卡"-style curation).
- The share of output that is AI-generated will be highest in volume-oriented, metric-verified or disposable content: live-ops events, ads, mini-games and UGC. It will be lowest in content that must be consistent, engine-integrated and emotionally credible.
