## 1. Supply vs demand: release volume, revenue distribution, player time and market growth (2015 → Sept 2026)

### Takeaway
From 2015 to 2025, Steam releases grew about 7x (2.8k → roughly 19.5–21k), and 2026 is on pace for 24–28k. Over the same years, PC/console play time has been flat or falling and the global market has grown in low single digits most years (+9% in 2025). The result is a median Steam game that earns less each year (12 straight years of decline), about half of 2025 releases with fewer than 10 reviews, the top 1% of titles taking about 85% of revenue, and new releases winning only about 13% of PC/console play time.

### Inferences
- **Supply is outgrowing demand.** Steam releases doubled from 2020 to 2025 (9,648 → about 20k) and grew about 7x from 2015. Meanwhile, Mature Market 8 PC/console software sales fell 8% (2021–24), global revenue was roughly flat 2021–24 before +9% in 2025, and PC/console play time was flat to down. Each additional title mostly divides a fixed pool of attention, which is exactly what a median decline in 12 consecutive years looks like.
- **A new game's main competitor is the back catalog, not other new games.** New titles get about 13% of PC/console play time and 21–29% of a year's Steam revenue, and that share is falling. Any AI-driven increase in supply enters the most crowded, lowest-share slice of the market.
- **Extra volume adds to the long tail, not the top.** The top-1% cut-off ($15.9M lifetime) is about 4,000x the median (under $4k). The top 20 games each gross over $50M a year and are mostly AAA, IP or evergreen live services, so extra supply mostly lengthens the tail.
- **Mobile is monetizing existing players.** Downloads are falling (−12% in H1 2026) while IAP revenue is roughly flat, and the number of new games reaching the US top 1,000 halved in five years. Revenue growth now comes from existing players, and new entrants face rising acquisition costs (see §3E).
- **The 2026 release surge is largely AI-enabled.** AI-disclosed games account for 60–90% of Steam's monthly release growth (see §4).

### Gaps
- There is no public, consistent time series of total PC play hours or global hours per week from 2015 to 2026. Newzoo, Circana (US only) and MIDiA use incompatible definitions.
- Newzoo's 2021 and 2022 global totals were not captured, and its 2023–2025 base years were revised several times. A clean 2021–2026 series needs Newzoo's latest report.
- China's 2022 figure (about ¥265.9B) is implied from the 2023 growth rate, not directly sourced.
- The 2026 year-to-date count (20,253 to Sept 20) comes from a single lesser-known tracker. Verify against SteamDB.
- The Game World Observer median figures are internally inconsistent, and newer cohorts are biased downward because they have had less time to earn.
- QuestMobile's time share for mobile games specifically (as opposed to all video) was not captured.

## 2. What drives willingness to pay and success: quality and polish, novelty, IP, social features, live ops, iteration, and small-team hits

### Takeaway
Revenue rewards relative quality steeply: small quality advantages near the top are magnified through reviews, concurrent players, streams and algorithms. Yet players say they mostly want familiar experiences, and hits are rare even for the best studios (Supercell launched about 1 in 7 titles; Voodoo about 0.4% of prototypes; Rovio's 52nd game was Angry Birds). The past decade's small-team hits were cheap to make — Vampire Survivors used £1,100 of assets, and PEAK sold 2M copies on under $200k. They won on a novel core loop, social or streamer appeal, and clear creative direction, not on production scale.

### Cited Findings

**Quality signals and sales**
- Binken & Stremersch (2009, *Journal of Marketing*) — [Journal of Marketing](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmkg.73.2.88); [EUR PDF](https://personal.eur.nl/stremersch/artikels/2009%20-%20JM%20-%20The%20Effect%20of%20Superstar%20Software%20on%20Hardware%20Sales%20in%20System%20Markets(JM%20Version).pdf):
  - Game software sales show *increasing returns to quality*: sales rise disproportionately with quality.
  - The distribution of quality is negatively skewed, so high quality is scarce.
  - A "superstar" title lifts console hardware sales by about 14% (about 167,000 units), for about five months.
- Evidence on Metacritic is mixed — [Wikipedia: Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic):
  - A 2013 study of 196 games found a "close link" between scores and sales.
  - A 2015 study of 88 Xbox 360 and 80 PS3 games (2012) found no effect.
  - Older industry data showed games scoring 90+ averaging about 800k units vs about 250k for games scoring 80–90 — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/84203/higher-metacritic-scores-impact-game-sales-positively)
  - An academic critique of Metacritic's validity as a measure of game value — [Eludamos](https://septentrio.uit.no/index.php/eludamos/article/download/vol7no1-6/7-1-6-html?inline=1)
- Ma (2025, *International Journal of Computer Games Technology*), a machine-learning study across Steam, Twitch and Metacritic — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/ijcg/5776632):
  - Steam follower counts, peak concurrent players and total reviews are the strongest revenue predictors, followed by Twitch viewer engagement.
  - Metacritic critic scores have a positive but minimal financial effect.
  - Early Access helps; supported languages, genre and multiplayer have weaker effects.
- Practitioner rule of thumb — [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/indie-game-revenue-data) (commercial blog; lower authority):
  - Reviews are the strongest predictor of lifetime revenue.
  - Games rated "Very Positive" or "Overwhelmingly Positive" earn more per review.
  - Each review represents about 20–60 sales.
  - The analysts' "Boxleiter" estimate is gross ≈ reviews × price × 35 — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/steam-games-that-openly-use-generative-ai-earned-usd660-million-this-year-including-call-of-duty-black-ops-6-stellaris-and-more-as-studios-continue-to-rely-on-the-technology/)

**Finding the fun: kill rates and prototypes per hit**
- Supercell — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/03/14/all-games-killed-by-supercell-everdale-hay-day-pop-clash-mini); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/maybe-it-s-time-to-kill-your-game-and-move-on-supercell-on-cutting-its-losses); [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/other/item-210):
  - CEO Ilkka Paananen said in 2023 that the company had killed more than 30 games to launch five.
  - By 2016 it had killed 14.
  - In one earlier stretch of 10 games, seven were killed as prototypes, two were killed in soft launch, and one (Clash Royale) launched globally.
  - Its small independent "cells" have the autonomy to kill their own games.

### Inferences
- **Quality pays through visibility.** Early reviews, concurrent players, followers and streams compound into algorithmic visibility, and returns to quality are convex (superstar economics). Being slightly better than the alternatives near the top is worth far more than being competent in the middle.
- **Demand favors "familiar plus one fresh twist".** Players say they want familiar experiences (Bain: two-thirds), yet breakout hits are often genre mash-ups or twists on familiar loops (poker-roguelike, a reversed bullet-hell, "survival-crafting with creature collecting"; these labels are my characterization).
- **Hit-making is a search process with very low base rates.** Supercell, Voodoo and Rovio all show that the scarce input is judgment about what is fun: taste, playtesting discipline, and the willingness to kill projects. Production capacity is not the constraint. Cheaper prototyping means more attempts, but the number of hits the market can absorb is set by attention, not by the number of attempts.
- **Production scale has not been the binding constraint for small teams for a decade.** Cheap engines, asset stores and Steam Direct were enough for solo developers and teams of 4–30 to reach $100M–$700M outcomes. Their edge was design novelty, social or co-op play, suitability for streaming, low prices and strong creative direction.
- **A clear audience and creative direction predict success.** Bain's 83% vs 50% gap supports this, but Bain's definitions and sample are not public.

### Gaps
- No systematic data was found on the share of development time or budget spent on polish (by genre or budget tier). Only practitioner framing exists.
- No rigorous causal estimate was found of Steam review score's effect on sales (for example, a regression discontinuity at the "Very Positive" threshold). Only correlations and practitioner multipliers exist.
- No quantitative study was found isolating the effect of a creative director or creative vision.
- Supercell's post-2023 launch outcomes (Squad Busters, mo.co) could not be verified.
- Palworld's 30.5M copies and $700M, and the lifetime Schedule I figures, are unverified estimates.
- Cox (2014), "What Makes a Blockbuster Video Game?" ([Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/mde.2608)), is relevant, but its findings were not captured.

## 3. Historical natural experiments: when production got cheaper, what happened to volume, quality, concentration and creator earnings

### Takeaway
Four natural experiments show the same pattern: free engines with an open Steam, the mobile hyper-casual boom, UGC platforms, and China's mini-games. Cheaper production causes a flood of supply. Median earnings collapse, concentration stays extreme, and the bottleneck moves to discovery and user acquisition, while platforms and a few hit-makers capture most of the value. Total market value followed demand and platform traffic, not supply. Where value creation depended on cheap distribution (hyper-casual ad arbitrage), a change in distribution (Apple's App Tracking Transparency, ATT) broke the model.

### Cited Findings

**A. Free engines, asset stores and an open Steam (2012–2018, and the long tail since)**
- At GDC in March 2015, both major engines dropped their upfront fees — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/unreal-engine-4-is-now-free/); [Unreal Engine blog](https://www.unrealengine.com/blog/ue4-is-free); [TweakTown](https://www.tweaktown.com/news/43898/unity-5-announced-royalty-free-personal-edition/index.html):
  - Unreal Engine 4 went free, with a 5% royalty after the first $3,000 per product per quarter.
  - Unity 5 Personal Edition became free and royalty-free for developers with under $100k of revenue or funding.
- Steam's "indiepocalypse" — [Game World Observer, 2021](https://gameworldobserver.com/2021/08/11/how-steam-stopped-indiepocalypse-but-almost-killed-small-games):
  - Greenlight (from about 2012–13) loosened curation, and Steam Direct ($100 fee, 2017) opened the store to all.
  - Monthly releases in 2015 were about three times the 2013 level, and 2016 releases were up 66% on 2015.
  - "Asset flips" (games assembled from Unity asset-store packs) and "achievement spam" games appeared.
  - Academic political-economy view (labor precarity among indies) — [Game Studies, Lipkin 2019](https://gamestudies.org/1902/articles/lipkin); counter-view — [Game Developer: "The 5 Myths of the Indiepocalypse"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-5-myths-of-the-indiepocalypse)
- Volume: 1,713 releases (2014) → 6,930 (2017) → 8,871 (2018) → 12,274 (2022) → about 20k (2025) — [SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/stats/releases/)
- Outcomes:
  - Median revenue per title has declined for 12 consecutive years, and the top 1% take about 85% — [Game World Observer 2026](https://gameworldobserver.com/2026/09/01/the-median-revenue-of-games-on-steam-has-been-declining-for-12-consecutive-years)
  - Steam's total gross nonetheless hit a record of about $16.9B in 2025 — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/who-won-steams-best-of-2025-revenue)
  - Indie mega-hits reached unprecedented absolute scale (§2).

**B. Mobile gold rush, then the hyper-casual boom and bust**
- Hyper-casual downloads, per the [Gamesforum Intelligence hyper-casual report (PDF)](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Gamesforum-Intelligence-Hypercasual-Gaming-Report.pdf):
  - Downloads grew 60% YoY in 2020 and 15% in 2021.
  - Downloads of the top 100 hyper-casual games peaked at more than 7B in 2020, fell by about 1B a year through 2021–22, and reached 4.4B in 2023.
  - The causes were post-pandemic behavior, plus Apple's IDFA/ATT privacy changes hitting CPIs, acquisition budgets and ad revenue. The model depended on video-ad monetization and cheap acquisition.
- Hyper-casual downloads were 3.5B in Q3 2023 (−8% QoQ, −12.5% YoY), after a −24% YoY fall in Q4 2022 — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/35-billion-hypercasual-games-installed-in-2023s-third-quarter/); [Mobile Marketing Reads](https://mobilemarketingreads.com/hypercasual-game-downloads-decline-8-in-q3-2023/)
- Voodoo — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/voodoo/); [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2024/6/3/voodoos-secret-sauce-from-0-to-250m-hybridcasual-revenue-in-3-years):
  - Alex Shea declared "Hypercasual is dead" at Pocket Gamer Connects London in 2023.
  - Hyper-casual was only about 25% of Voodoo's revenue by 2023. Total revenue was about $570M (+14%), driven by the pivot to hybrid-casual, which reached $250M of revenue in three years.
  - Voodoo cut its studio network from about 700 studios to about 100 teams.
- Mobile saturation today: new games in the US top 1,000 by downloads halved in five years (about 200 → just over 100 in 2024), and games more than two years old dominate downloads — [GameDev Reports: Sensor Tower 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-mobile-market-in-2025)

**C. UGC platforms (Roblox, Fortnite/UEFN)**
- Roblox Developer Exchange (DevEx) payouts, 12 months to June 30, 2026 — [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/roblox-top-creators-devex-payouts-2026-1819403); [iXBT](https://ixbt.games/en/news/2026/09/14/435627-roblox-raskryla-doxody-sozdatelei-kontenta.html):

  - The top 10 creators averaged $65.7M each (about $657M, or about 39% of the pool).
  - The top 100 averaged $10.8M and the top 1,000 averaged $1.4M.
  - More than 42,000 creators took part; the median payout was about $1,500.
- Roblox, calendar 2025: about $1.5B paid to creators, with $477M in Q4 (+70% YoY) — [LinkedIn post by Roblox's Hari Iyer](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hariiyer_for-all-of-2025-creators-earned-over-15-activity-7425696253142933506-MbKG). In an earlier period (DevEx over $1B), the top 10 averaged $33.9M — [SQ Magazine](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/roblox-game-creation-and-monetization-statistics/)
- Roblox engagement concentration:
  - 50% of in-experience hours were spent in the top 50 experiences (month of Dec 2024) — [Roblox FY2024 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000155837025004965/rblx-20250331xars.pdf)

  - Creator islands reached 47% of hours by May 2026, up from 38% a year earlier — [Tech Insider CA](https://tech-insider.org/ca/fortnite-creator-economy-1-billion-2026/)
- Fortnite payouts:
  - $352M in 2024, about $370M in 2025, and more than $1B in total since 2023 — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/06/17/epic-games-unreal-editor-for-fortnite-creator-payouts/)

  - At the program's launch, Epic projected that more than 200 creators would earn over $100k a year — [Fortnite engagement payout update](https://www.fortnite.com/news/fortnite-engagement-payout-update?lang=en-US)

**D. China: WeChat and Douyin mini-games (微信/抖音小游戏)**

  - Monthly active users exceed 500M.
  - Developers exceeded 400k (January 2026) and later 500k; 80% are teams of 30 or fewer.

  - Creative homogenization (创意同质化) is pushing up acquisition costs.
  - The average ad creative lasted 5.2 days in 2024.
  - Acquiring one user costs about ¥98 for a casual mini-game and up to ¥4,000 for a flagship strategy (SLG) title.
  - Daily mini-game ad spend on the three major media platforms is about ¥144M.

**E. User acquisition as the bottleneck**
- AppsFlyer, *State of Gaming App Marketing 2026* — [AppsFlyer press release](https://www.appsflyer.com/company/newsroom/pr/gaming-marketing/); [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appsflyer-the-state-of-the-mobile):
  - 2025 gaming acquisition spend was about $25B (+3.8%); about half came from the US, and casual games took half.
  - Chinese publishers' acquisition spend outside China rose 22%, to a 35% share.
  - Top advertisers produced 2,400–2,600 creative variations per quarter (+25–30% YoY) as AI tools spread.
  - The paid-install share rose 10% and ad impressions rose 20%, so more ads competed for flat player attention. AppsFlyer frames the problem as "not a shortage of creativity, but a surplus of it".
- Vendor CPI benchmarks (lower authority; verify) — [Audiencelab](https://audiencelab.ai/blog/ios-cpi-trends-2025); [Mapendo](https://mapendo.co/blog/mobile-games-cpi-2025):
  - iOS gaming CPI averaged about $3.15, +12% vs 2024.
  - European gaming CPI rose 47% ($0.36 → $0.53).
  - Hybrid-casual CPI on Android rose from $0.54 to $0.95 between 2024 and 2025.

### Inferences
- **The pattern is robust.** Four cheaper-production shocks each produced (1) a surge in releases, (2) a collapse in median and typical earnings, (3) extreme concentration that persists or grows, and (4) a shift of the bottleneck to discovery and acquisition. Total market value followed demand and platform traffic, not supply.
- **Hyper-casual is the closest analog to AI mass production.** Its production was extremely cheap (about 1,000 prototypes a year at Voodoo), and its value rested on acquisition arbitrage rather than intrinsic retention. When distribution economics changed (ATT, plus post-pandemic demand), the category collapsed, and the survivors moved up-market to hybrid-casual depth, retention and IAP. AI-mass-produced games that rely on paid or algorithmic traffic face the same squeeze.
- **UGC platforms show cheap production can enlarge a platform's value when the platform owns discovery and pays by engagement** (Roblox payouts up about 50% a year; Fortnite creator share 36.5% → 47%). But:
  - Some of that growth is reallocation from other games: Roblox play time rose 52% while total PC/console play time fell.
  - Creator earnings are extremely skewed. Computing from the averages above, Roblox's top 10 took about 39% of DevEx, the top 100 about 64%, and the top 1,000 (about 2.4% of participants) about 82%, while the median creator earned about $1,500.
  - Most of the value accrues to the platform.
- **China's mini-games repeat the Steam pattern in about two years.** Growth came from platform traffic and hybrid monetization, then homogenization, five-day creative lifespans and rising acquisition costs followed. The platform now subsidizes new, higher-quality launches (up to ¥70M) to fight homogenization, which suggests curation and quality are becoming scarce again.
- **User acquisition is where AI shows up first, and so far it raises costs rather than lowering them.** Cheaper creative production multiplies ad volume, so rivals compete for the same impressions. This is a Red Queen dynamic, where everyone runs faster just to stay in place.

### Gaps
- No reliable time series was found for the number of games on the App Store or Google Play, nor for mobile revenue concentration in 2010–2016. The mobile gold-rush analog therefore rests on hyper-casual data and recent Sensor Tower saturation data.
- No data was captured on how the *quality* distribution changed over time (for example, mean Steam review score by cohort, or the share of releases rated "Very Positive").
- Fortnite's concentration by island (the top-N islands' share of creator play time or payouts) was not captured.
- Douyin mini-game data (volume, revenue, homogenization) was not captured separately.
- Steam's median revenue per title around 2013–2015 (before the flood) was not captured in absolute terms; only the direction of the 12-year decline was.

## 4. AI-specific evidence: player attitudes, how AI-disclosed games perform on Steam, and whether AI-native games monetize

### Takeaway
Player-facing generative AI currently carries a measurable demand penalty with core PC audiences. AI-disclosed games get about 53% fewer reviews in matched comparisons and a 17.9-point lower recommendation rate than games using procedural generation, and in 2026 they were about one third of Steam releases but only 10–17% of sales in recent quarters. Only about 25% of US players say AI makes them less likely to buy, and about 8% of engaged Steam users would refuse. Invisible uses (voice for minor lines, localization) show up among successes, while AI visuals dominate among failures. AI-native products are real but tiny: AI companion apps took about $120M in 2025, against a $201.6B games market.

### Cited Findings

**Player attitudes (global)**
- Quantic Foundry (Oct–Dec 2025; N = 1,799; opt-in respondents to its Gamer Motivation Profile) — [Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/); [GamesMarket](https://www.gamesmarket.global/quantic-foundry-most-gamers-are-negative-about-the-use-of-gen-ai-in-video-games/):
  - 85% are negative about generative AI in games, and 63% chose the most negative option.
  - That is more negative than attitudes to blockchain games (79% negative in 2024).
  - Women and non-binary gamers are more negative than men; older gamers are more favorable and younger gamers more negative.
  - Nick Yee: "Such a highly-skewed negative response is rare."
- Circana PlayerPulse (US; Dec 2025) — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/genai-in-games-most-players-just-dont-care-study-finds/1100-6538972/):
  - Just over 25% said generative AI in a game would make them "less likely" to buy it or would make them refuse, up from about 22% in March 2024.
  - Fewer than 10% said it would make them more interested, and more than 50% were neutral or don't care much.
- GameDiscoverCo survey (about 3,800 engaged Steam users; self-selected) — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-do-steam-fans-really-think-about); [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/survey-finds-only-31-percent-of-steam-users-have-a-problem-with-ai-in-games-with-43-percent-totally-fine-with-it/):
  - 23.4% have no problem with AI and 19.6% are fine with it (43% combined).
  - 25.6% are neutral, 23.3% are "not keen", and 8.1% refuse to buy.
- Bain 2026: 42% of gamers are more comfortable with AI in games than a year ago, rising to 59% of teens aged 13–17 — [Bain](https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/2026/gamings-next-winners-will-be-built-on-player-focus-ai-powered-personalization-and-direct-relationships-bain-co-report-finds/)
- MIDiA surveyed a representative 6,300 gamers on generative AI and purchase decisions, but the headline figures were not captured — [MIDiA](https://www.midiaresearch.com/blog/new-research-what-do-gamers-really-think-about-generative-ai-in-games)
- China: no rigorous Chinese player survey was found. Media coverage (for example GamerSky's March 2025 piece on player complaints about big studios using AI content) shows pushback but gives no representative numbers — [GamerSky](https://www.gamersky.com/news/202503/1891688.shtml); [Sohu](https://www.sohu.com/a/1043386613_121984121)

### Inferences
- **Visible generative-AI content is a demand tax, not a ceiling.** It cuts visibility (fewer reviews), lowers scores and signals low effort. The tax falls hardest on marginal and "high-potential" games; a strong game like ARC Raiders can overcome it. Invisible uses (code, voice for minor lines, localization, tools) carry no detectable penalty. Confidence: medium-high for PC/Steam, low for mobile and China, where data is thin.
- **The attitude data describe an intense minority and an indifferent majority.** About 25% are less likely to buy and about 8% refuse, but these are disproportionately the engaged players who write reviews and drive word of mouth, which feed the visibility algorithms in §2. A penalty confined to a minority can therefore have an outsized effect on discovery.
- **AI-assisted releases are growing much faster than the revenue they earn.** They went from about 11% to about 31% of releases in two years, while their sales share is usually below their release share except when a hit lands. That is consistent with AI mainly lowering the cost of entry into the long tail, adding to the §1 dilution.
- **AI-native experiences monetize weakly so far.** Companion apps' roughly $120M a year is about 0.06% of the $201.6B games market. Viral AI party games show reach, not revenue per player. The strongest signals are social or platform-embedded (Discord) and gacha-style monetization of AI companions (Talkie), not premium PC.
- **The direction of attitudes is unclear.** Bain finds rising comfort, especially among teens; Quantic finds younger gamers more negative. These conflict, possibly because of different samples. Confidence: low.

### Gaps
- No representative Chinese player survey on AI-generated game content was found.
- There is no AI-disclosure regime or performance data for mobile app stores or WeChat mini-games, so the penalty cannot be measured there.
- MIDiA's figures for its 6,300-gamer survey were not captured.
- Revenue for most AI-native games (Whispers from the Star, AI-NPC indies) is undisclosed.
- None of the Steam studies establishes causality cleanly. Selection is a confounder: low-budget developers are more likely to use AI.
- The post-January 2026 disclosure regime (player-facing only) breaks comparability with 2024–25 disclosure shares.

## 5. Cost structure vs value: when players pay for content volume, and where AI can cut cost without cutting perceived quality

### Takeaway
Content volume is what players pay for mainly in live-service and gacha games, where new characters, events and regions drive spending, and it is a large fixed cost (Genshin Impact: about $200M a year to support six-week updates, per its founder in 2021). In premium games, most players never finish the content (about 14% average completion in one achievement study), and the best-selling small games are cheap. Value sits in the core loop, the first hours and social replay, not in content quantity. AI can cut cost without cutting perceived quality mainly where players don't look closely. It risks commoditization in visible art and narrative, and in template-driven segments.

### Cited Findings
- Genshin Impact — [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/genshin-impact-was-such-a-hit-that-its-going-to-cost-the-studio-twice-as-much-as-the-initial-budget-yearly-to-support-it/); [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/genshin-impact-most-expensive-game-ever-made/):
  - Initial development budget of about $100M.
  - In Feb 2021, miHoYo president Cai Haoyu estimated about $200M a year of ongoing development to sustain content updates every six weeks.
  - Estimated cumulative spend was about $500M by the second anniversary.
  - The money goes to new characters, events, regions and main story.
- Live-service demand risk:
  - Concord launched Aug 23, 2024, and was shut down within about two weeks; developer Firewalk was closed in Oct 2024 — [Variety](https://variety.com/2024/digital/news/playstation-shuts-down-concord-refund-players-1236128208/); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/paultassi/2024/10/29/sony-shuts-down-concord-developer-no-relaunch-coming/); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/concord-shutting-down-closing-servers-refunds-ps5-1851638566)
  - Reported figures of about 25,000 sales and a development deal of about $200M come from low-authority recaps (verify).
  - More than half of 2025's live-service launches lost over 90% of their players — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/2025-live-service-games-player-analysis/)
- Players rarely finish premium content:
  - An achievement-data study found the average completion rate for primary single-player content was 14% (95% CI 13–15%) — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1875952118300181)
  - In one achievement dataset, nearly half the games (202) had 10% or fewer players finishing, and only about 2% (9) had more than half — [Death is a Whale](https://deathisawhale.com/2021/01/20/how-many-players-actually-finish-games/)
  - Short, linear games are finished more often (The Stanley Parable about 74%; Resident Evil Village about 58%) — [Two Average Gamers](https://www.twoaveragegamers.com/game-completion-rates/) (small sample)
- Spending patterns:
  - B2P play time grew and F2P play time fell in 2025 — [GameDev Reports: Newzoo](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/newzoo-pc-and-console-market-in-2026)
  - Newzoo expects growth to come from competitive communities, premium releases, mobile monetization and long-lived franchises — [Game Wisdom](https://game-wisdom.com/general/global-games-market-grows-toward-213-9-billion)
- Cheap games regularly top the charts: Clair Obscur (under $10M), Palworld (about $6.7M), PEAK (under $200k for 2M sales) and Vampire Survivors (about £1,100 of assets) — see §2 sources.
- Bain on AI and cost: with a clear target player, AI enables "faster prototyping and cheaper iteration"; without that focus "AI does not lower risk… it simply enables studios to scale a bad bet more quickly" — [Bain](https://www.bain.com/about/media-center/press-releases/2026/gamings-next-winners-will-be-built-on-player-focus-ai-powered-personalization-and-direct-relationships-bain-co-report-finds/); [Consultancy-me](https://www.consultancy-me.com/news/14291/video-game-success-is-shifting-from-mass-appeal-to-superfans)

  - Successful AI-flagged Steam games over-index on voice and localization; failures over-index on AI visuals (72%) — [Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/news/2026-07-20-steam-ai-disclosure-study)

### Inferences
- **Where content volume matters (live service, gacha, UGC platforms):**
  - Spending is tied to a steady stream of new characters, events and seasons, so per-unit content cost is a large and direct lever. Genshin-scale spending is about $200M a year; applying that to a game grossing well over $1B a year puts content at roughly 10–20% of revenue. That ratio is my estimate, not a sourced figure.
  - AI could lower the cost per character or event, but three things limit the net gain:
    - Perceived craft is part of what players pay for when they buy premium characters.
    - Every competitor gets the same tools, so a Red Queen dynamic is likely to compete savings away into bigger or more frequent content.
    - More content can inflate the supply of characters and erode their scarcity value.
  - Expect modest margin gains for leaders, not a cost collapse. Confidence: medium-low.
- **Where content volume matters little (premium single-player and session-based multiplayer):**
  - Most buyers never finish, so marginal content has low marginal value.
  - Value is concentrated in the core loop, the first hours, feel and polish, and social replay (co-op or party play, roguelike replayability).
  - Evidence: small-budget hits dominate breakout lists, and short games get finished. Confidence: medium-high.
- **Where AI can cut cost without cutting perceived quality:**
  - Tasks with low player salience or that players don't see at all: code and tooling, QA, localization, secondary voice lines, data and live-ops analytics, prototyping and greyboxing.
  - Also first drafts of content that humans then art-direct.
  - Haro's success and failure data and Valve's exemption for developer tools both point here. Confidence: medium.
- **Where AI risks commoditization or backlash:**
  - Visible hero art, a signature art style, key narrative and lead voice acting (the stigma data in §4).
  - Template-driven segments (hyper-casual, 换皮 reskins, mini-game clones, "Vampire Survivors–likes"), where AI speeds imitation. Novelty then decays faster and homogenization raises acquisition costs.
  - Ad creatives, where AI volume raises competition rather than efficiency. Confidence: medium.
- **The expensive failure mode is misjudging demand.** Concord and the collapse of 2025 live-service launches came from misreading what players wanted, not from production cost. AI lowers the cost of failing but not its probability, so Bain's warning that AI "scales the wrong bets" applies.

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) primary data was found on miHoYo's content spend per version, cost per character, or Genshin's revenue trend. Chinese community claims seen in search, such as miHoYo 2025 R&D of ¥12.8B and Genshin monthly revenue in July 2026, could not be verified and were excluded.
- No P&L breakdown was found showing content cost as a share of revenue for major live-service or gacha titles.
- No documented case was found of AI measurably lowering gacha or live-service content cost at shipped-quality standards. Production-side cases are covered in the parallel notes on AI adoption in game production.

## 6. Synthesis: how value is created in games vs software, and what that implies for AI's impact on game-industry value (with confidence levels)

### Takeaway
In games, value is created by scarce and relative things: novel, well-tuned fun, polish, IP, social context and sustained live operations. These are sold into a time budget that is flat or shrinking, through platforms whose discovery and acquisition costs are the binding constraint. Production capacity has not been the bottleneck for a decade. So AI's production gains will turn into more titles and some cost and margin savings much more readily than into industry value. The main exceptions are faster iteration for teams with good taste, invisible-task savings, UGC platforms, and a still-small AI-native category.

### Cited Findings
Anchor facts for the synthesis, restated from §§1–5:
- **Supply far outpaces demand:**
  - Steam releases went from 2,822 (2015) to about 20k (2025), and 2026 is on pace for 24–28k — [SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/stats/releases/); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-averages-almost-70-new-games-a-day-in-2026.1374113.0.html)
  - Global games revenue is $201.6B in 2025 (+9.1%) and forecast at $213.9B in 2026 (+6.1%) — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/global-games-revenue-breached-200b-in-2025-newzoo/); [Game Wisdom](https://game-wisdom.com/general/global-games-market-grows-toward-213-9-billion)
  - PC/console play time fell 1% in 2025 — [GameDev Reports: Newzoo](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/newzoo-pc-and-console-market-in-2026)
- **Winner-take-most distribution:** the top 1% take 84.5% of Steam revenue, and the median paid game makes under $4k — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/behold-the-whole-history-of-steams-economy-in-one-picture-the-top-1-percent-of-games-earn-84-5-percent-of-estimated-revenue-and-most-games-barely-make-anything/)
- **Old games dominate:** new releases got 21% of Steam's H1 2026 revenue — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/113102-steam-made-11-billion-last-6-months-most.html)
- **Increasing returns to quality** — [Binken & Stremersch 2009](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmkg.73.2.88)
- **Hit base rates are low:** Supercell killed 30+ games to launch 5, and Voodoo launches about 0.4% of prototypes — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/03/14/all-games-killed-by-supercell-everdale-hay-day-pop-clash-mini); [Voodoo](https://voodoo.io/news/voodoo-s-secret-sauce-from-0-to-250m-hybridcasual-revenue-in-3-years)
- **AI is already driving supply but not revenue:**
  - AI-flagged games are 30.8% of 2026 Steam releases and 60–90% of release growth, but only 10–17% of sales in H1 2026 — [Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/news/2026-07-20-steam-ai-disclosure-study)
  - AI-disclosed games get about 53% fewer reviews in matched comparisons — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/data-analyst-finds-ai-stigma-on-steam-can-reduce-the-number-of-reviews-a-game-gets-by-around-53-percent-and-the-reviews-it-does-get-are-more-negative/)
- **AI raises marketing competition:** creative variants +25–30% and impressions +20% — [AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/company/newsroom/pr/gaming-marketing/)
- **UGC concentrates value at the platform and the top:** Roblox's top 10 creators take about 39% of about $1.7B in DevEx, and the median payout is about $1,500 — [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/roblox-top-creators-devex-payouts-2026-1819403)
- **AI-native revenue is small:** AI companion apps were on track for about $120M in 2025 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/12/ai-companion-apps-on-track-to-pull-in-120m-in-2025)

### Inferences

**How value is created: games vs software**

The software column is an analytical characterization for comparison. It was not researched in this note; cross-check it against the coding notes in this folder.

| Dimension | Software (esp. business and productivity) | Games |
|---|---|---|
| Nature of demand | Derived from productive tasks. Latent demand for custom tools and automation expands as building gets cheaper. Many tools can run at once, often without human attention. | Final consumption bounded by leisure time and attention, which are flat or falling in mature markets (Newzoo, Circana, Ball). Games substitute for each other hour for hour. |
| What users pay for | Function, correctness, integration, reliability. "Good enough" code creates value, and more functionality adds to it. | Relative experience quality: novel fun, feel and polish, IP and identity, friends playing, ongoing events. Value is relative to the best alternative for the next hour, and novelty decays as it is imitated. |
| Verifying quality | Largely testable (specs, tests, benchmarks), so AI output can be checked mechanically. | "Fun" emerges from play and must be found through iteration and playtesting. Kill decisions need taste. Hard to verify automatically. |
| Market structure | Many sustainable niches; switching costs and lock-in; much value is captured by users rather than sellers (internal tools). | Extreme power law (top 1% ≈ 85% of Steam revenue). Discovery and acquisition are the bottleneck, and platforms take a large cut. |
| Reaction to visible AI | Users are largely indifferent to whether code was AI-written. | A measurable penalty for visible AI content in core segments (53% fewer reviews; a 17.9-point lower recommendation rate). |
| What cheaper production has done historically | More software gets built and used, expanding value (assumption). | More titles and lower medians. Value shifted to platforms, hit-makers, IP holders and acquisition-savvy firms (Steam 2015+, hyper-casual, mini-games, UGC). |

**What this implies for AI's effect on game-industry value** (to about 2030; confidence in brackets)

1. **Release volume keeps rising, driven by AI-assisted entrants** [High]. Steam is likely to exceed 24k releases in 2026, with more than a third AI-flagged under the player-facing definition. The same will happen on mini-game and UGC platforms.
2. **The median title keeps earning less, and at least about half of releases keep drawing fewer than 10 reviews** [High]. Mechanism: a fixed attention pool, a dominant back catalog, and the visibility penalty on AI-disclosed games.
3. **Revenue concentration stays extreme (top 1% at roughly 80% or more)** [High]. AI mostly lengthens the long tail and does not dislodge IP and live-service incumbents. Small-team hits will continue, but they were already possible before AI.
4. **Total industry revenue stays demand-bound** [Medium-high]. Growth tracks time, spending power, pricing, platform shifts and marquee releases such as GTA VI, at roughly Newzoo's 3–7% a year. AI adds little to top-line revenue through about 2028 unless an AI-native category breaks out.
5. **AI's value shows up mainly as cost and margin in tasks players don't see** [Medium]. That means code, QA, localization, secondary voice, tools, analytics and prototyping. Part of the saving will be competed away into bigger scope, faster content cadence and marketing spend (the Red Queen dynamic). The durable gains go to:
   - platforms (Valve, Roblox, Epic, Tencent/WeChat, Apple and Google), which collect a toll on the extra volume;
   - IP owners and proven hit-makers, who can reinvest savings into polish and live ops;
   - publishers skilled at user acquisition.
6. **Discovery and acquisition costs keep inflating** [Medium]. AI multiplies ad creatives and store pages but not player attention. Evidence: impressions +20%, creative volume +25–30%, five-day creative lifespans in China, rising CPIs.
7. **Faster iteration can raise quality and hit rates per dollar for teams with taste** [Low–Medium]. More prototypes per hit (the Supercell and Voodoo model) and cheaper pivots could improve the quality of the games that do ship. But the number of hits the market can absorb is set by attention, so this redistributes share toward better-run teams more than it grows the pie.
8. **Visible generative-AI content keeps carrying a penalty with core PC and console audiences through at least 2027** [Medium]. The penalty is likely smaller in mobile, casual and China mini-games, but data there is thin [Low]. Players may accept it more over time (Bain: rising comfort among teens), but survey signals conflict.
9. **Live-service and gacha content treadmills are where AI cost savings matter most economically** [Medium-low]. The likely result is a "quality arms race" with more, faster content, not lower prices or higher industry revenue. Players pay for perceived craft and scarcity, so AI-bulked content risks commoditizing characters and events.
10. **AI-native experiences (LLM-driven NPCs, companions, generative UGC) stay under about 1% of game revenue through about 2028** [Medium for size]. A breakout is more likely through social and UGC platforms (Discord, Roblox-style creation, companion "gacha") than through premium PC games [Low for form]. This is the one channel through which AI could expand demand rather than only supply, so it is the key long-run uncertainty.

**Bottom line for the comparison with software:**
- *Conversion of production gains into value.* In software, cheaper production plausibly converts into more delivered value, because demand is productive and latent. In games, the same gains convert mostly into more competition for a fixed attention budget, so the ratio of production gains to industry value is much lower. The history of games shows no case where a production-cost shock by itself enlarged total player spending; UGC platforms grew partly by taking time from other games.
- *Where AI adds game value.* It adds value where it:
  - improves what players actually experience: faster iteration toward fun, better localization reaching new markets, more responsive live ops;
  - enables genuinely new, social or personalized experiences;
  - cuts costs players never see.
- *Where AI destroys value.* It destroys value where it signals low effort, or where it floods template-driven segments with lookalike content.

### Gaps
- There is no causal study of how production-cost shocks affect *total* player spending, as opposed to supply and distribution. The historical analogs are descriptive.
- There are no Chinese or mobile equivalents of the Steam AI-disclosure performance data, and no representative Chinese player survey on AI.
- The software side of the comparison is characterized analytically here. The report writer should anchor it with the coding-adoption and productivity notes in this folder.
- Measures of player time budget are fragmented (Newzoo is proprietary and revised; Circana covers the US only; MIDiA and QuestMobile use different definitions). A clean 2015–2026 global series of hours per player does not appear to exist publicly.
- Confidence labels reflect the evidence as of Sept 2026. The biggest swing factors are (a) whether an AI-native category finds a scalable monetization model, and (b) whether player attitudes normalize as AI use becomes universal and invisible under Valve's narrower disclosure rule.
