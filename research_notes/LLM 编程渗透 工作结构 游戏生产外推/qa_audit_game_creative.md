- `game_industry_ai_adoption_pipeline.md` (below: **N1**)
- `game_ai_opportunities_frontier.md` (**N2**)
- `creative_content_industries_analog.md` (**N3**)

**Date.** 2026-09-28.

**Method.** All three notes read in full. ~60 load-bearing quantitative claims extracted. Verification attempted in this order: (1) WebSearch — only **4 queries** went through before the session-wide 200-call budget was exhausted; (2) WebFetch/curl — every domain in the notes' source lists is denied at the proxy (CONNECT 403; confirmed via `/__agentproxy/status`), except anthropic.com, claude.com, microsoft.com, cloud.google.com and github.com; I used microsoft.com (Muse/WHAMM pages) and the GitHub search API (repo creation dates) for the handful of claims those can settle; (3) internal-consistency and arithmetic checks across the three notes; (4) definition/denominator analysis.

**Verification limits — read this before using the tables.** Only ~12 claims could be checked against an external source this session. Everything else is marked **UNVERIFIABLE** with a sub-tag: **[U-consistent]** = internally consistent, consistent with the auditor's background knowledge and with ≥2 independent-looking sources in the note → the report may use it "as reported", with the caveat noted; **[U-weak]** = single or low-quality source → cite only with an explicit caveat, or not at all; **[U-no]** = must not be used. I did not "correct" any figure from memory alone; where my background knowledge conflicts with a note, the row says so and the item goes to the VERIFICATION QUEUE at the end for the coordinator to run.

## 1. Overall verdict per note

**N1 — game_industry_ai_adoption_pipeline.md.** Reliability: **medium-high on the survey layer, medium on China, low-medium on cost structure.** The note's best feature is that it separates the three measurement layers (studio-level "any AI use", worker-level personal use, and player-facing Steam disclosure) and flags vendor bias; the four searches I could run confirmed its GDC 2024/2025/2026 headline figures (31% personal use in 2024, 52% company-level in 2025, 36% personal use and 52% negative sentiment in 2026). Main weaknesses: (a) it misses that GDC 2024's 31% and GDC 2026's 36% are the *same* metric (personal use) and therefore form the only clean adoption time series — and that series is nearly flat (+5 pts in two years), which is the single most important finding for the user's question and the note buries it; (b) it repeats Unity's "77% drop in development time" without the definition — the figure is Unity telemetry on *median project editor time* (91 h → 21 h, Jan 2022–Dec 2025), not AI-attributed schedule compression (see row 9); (c) the Steam counts mix populations (all store pages with a disclosure, including unreleased and back-catalog pages, vs. share of a year's releases) and the "+681%/octupled" growth compares stock at two dates; (d) the cost-structure section rests entirely on outsourcing-vendor blog rules of thumb with mixed denominators (total budget incl. marketing vs. development budget) and is then used for arithmetic; (e) China efficiency figures are all company-claimed or self-perceived, which the note says, but its Takeaway still headlines them as if they were measurements; (f) "Big Chinese firms are not cutting R&D" over-reads group-wide R&D spend as evidence about game headcount.

**N2 — game_ai_opportunities_frontier.md.** Reliability: **medium on the company/financial anchors, low on anything from aggregator sites.** The note is candid about its search-budget failure and labels leads well. The GitHub checks I ran confirm its Tencent open-source timeline (HunyuanWorld-1.0 Jul 2025; Hunyuan-GameCraft code Aug 2025 — so the "still scheduled" summary it worried about is wrong; HY-World-2.0 Apr 2026) and Roblox Cube's open-sourcing (Mar 2025); Microsoft Research confirms Muse (19 Feb 2025) and WHAMM (4 Apr 2025). Main weaknesses: (a) heavy reliance on low-quality outlets (Tech-Insider, Outlook Respawn, Cryptopolitan, NoSmokeSport, Tekkie Pinas, StartupHub, AI CERTs, Postbeam, TradersUnion, SQ Magazine, IntelPilot, Yinji, explainx) for numbers that then appear in the opportunity map; (b) the Rec Room "AI cost > subscription revenue" detail, which the note itself says is unconfirmed, is nonetheless used as *the* cautionary case in three places; (c) the Takeaway of §1 says Fortnite's creator NPCs "reach tens of millions of players" — no usage figure for "Conversations" is given anywhere; (d) the Newzoo +6.1% vs. the ~$189B 2025 base is a real inconsistency the note flags but still headlines; (e) the "Steam 2026 pace ≈ 24K (+23%)" is a half-year annualization; (f) financing figures (Meshy $400M at $1.5B, Decart $300M at $4B, General Intuition $320M at $2.3B) come from press releases/secondary trackers and cannot be checked this session — they are plausible but the map's "Evidence: Strong on capital" grading depends on them.

**N3 — creative_content_industries_analog.md.** Reliability: **high on the Western academic/platform layer, medium-low on the China 2026 layer.** The evidence-tagging discipline ([Measured]/[Survey]/[Anecdote]/[Vendor]) is the best in the folder and the supply-vs-consumption distinction (Deezer uploads vs. streams) is exactly the frame the user's "volume ≠ value" point needs. The academic effect sizes (Hui et al. −2%/−5.2%; Demirci et al. −21%; Teutloff et al. −20–50%; Frey & Llanos-Paredes 0.71 pp/28,000; Zhou & Lee +25%/+50%; CVL 21.4%/118,500) match the auditor's knowledge of those papers but could not be re-verified this session. Main weaknesses: (a) the China short-drama section leans on a Zhihu summary of an industry report, a Weibo post and a headline ("98.7% lose money"), and its Takeaway generalizes Douyin upload counts (April 2026: 44,200 AI/comic vs. 3,248 live-action) into "the large majority of new micro-drama releases" — a platform-and-format-specific upload ratio, not a national release share; (b) the Gamma Data 2026 "−35% demand for traditional art roles" and the outsourcer anecdotes come from a single Sohu compilation the note itself suspects recycles 2023 press; (c) some tags are generous — the TAC translation report is an association *survey of firms*, not administrative data; (d) the Bloomberry "video editing +39%" complementary-demand finding is dated (through Feb 2024, before mature video models) and should not be presented as current; (e) the tier model is well argued but is inference; the report should present it as a framework, not a finding.

## 3. Definition and denominator warnings

1. **Four different "adoption rates" are not one curve.** (i) *Studio/company-level any-AI use* (Unity 62%→96%→95%; Google 90%; a16z 87%; 伽马数据 >99%) — vendor or trade samples, saturated by 2025, no longer informative. (ii) *Company-level in an independent worker survey* (GDC 2025: 52%). (iii) *Individual personal use at work* (GDC 2024: 31% → GDC 2026: 36%; and within that, studio staff 30% vs publisher/support/marketing staff 58%). (iv) *Player-facing shipped content* (Steam disclosures: ~20% of 2025 releases, ~31% of 2026 YTD releases). The report must label which layer every number belongs to; the user's coding comparison (developer-level adoption) maps to layer (iii) only.
2. **Unity 2026 "95%" = 100% − 5% "would not use"**; it can include intention, and the sample is ~300 Unity users. Unity 2025's "96%" was "use AI tools in select workflows" with "<half in any one workflow". Neither is comparable to GDC's personal-use figure.
3. **Unity "77% less development time"** = median *project editor time* (91 h → 21 h) across all Unity projects, Jan 2022–Dec 2025. Composition effect (many tiny projects), not AI productivity.
4. **Task percentages are conditional on being an AI user.** GDC 2026's "code assistance 47%" is 47% of the 36% who use gen AI ≈ 17% of all respondents. Unity's 62% coding is among AI-adopting Unity respondents.
5. **Steam counts have three populations:** (a) all store pages carrying a disclosure at a date (7,818 ≈ 7% mid-2025; 10,258 ≈ 8% ~H1 2026) — includes unreleased and back-catalog pages; (b) share of a year's *releases* (Haro 10.9/19.9/30.8%; Totally Human ~20% for 2025); (c) share of the *whole catalog*. The "+681%/octupled" compares (a) at two dates. Since Jan 2026 the form covers only player-consumed content and adds runtime-generation and a two-tier structure, so 2026 vs 2024–25 shares are not strictly comparable; coding-tool use is invisible in all Steam data.
6. **Steam disclosure is self-reported and honor-system** → lower bound for player-facing gen AI, and zero information about back-end use.
7. **Supply share ≠ consumption share.** Deezer: >50% of uploads but 1–3% of streams (85% of those fraudulent). Adobe Stock: ~48% of *inventory*, no sales share. Steam: ~20–31% of releases, but ~$0.66B cumulative estimated gross for all AI-disclosed titles vs ~$10B+/yr estimated Steam gross. Douyin: AI/comic *uploads* dominate; no view/revenue share except the 515.7B-views figure from the same secondary source.
8. **China adoption figures come from tiny, large-firm samples** (游戏工委: 22 firms, 90.9% >¥100M revenue; 伽马数据: practitioners at surveyed firms). Small-studio China adoption is unmeasured.
9. **"AI-generated share of assets" (37 Games >80% of 2D) is undefined**: almost certainly "AI-involved then human-refined", not "shipped untouched".
10. **Efficiency claims are task-level throughput** (hours per model, days per city, % of concept-art hours) from internal tests or perception surveys — none is a project-level cost or headcount outcome. Converting them to budget impact requires the task's share of the pipeline and rework/review cost, which no source gives.
11. **Budget shares mix denominators**: vendor rules of thumb are shares of *total* budget (incl. marketing); leaked figures (Spider-Man 2 $315M, Horizon FW $212M) are *development* budgets; Cyberpunk's ~$316M includes marketing.
12. **Layoff trackers differ in scope** (studio closures, contractor cuts, non-game divisions) and cannot be summed across trackers; "AI-attributed" layoffs in the folder are anonymous-source reports (King) or the note's own inference (Virtuos, Keywords).
13. **Freelance-platform studies measure postings and platform earnings**, not employment; windows end 2023–early 2024.
14. **Sentiment metrics differ**: GDC asks about impact *on the industry* (18→30→52% negative); Unity asks how respondents *feel about using AI* (79% positive); Google asks whether AI is "reshaping the industry" (97%). These are not contradictions; they are different questions asked of different samples.
15. **Vendor-sponsored surveys presented as neutral in places**: Unity (engine + AI seller), Google Cloud/Harris (AI seller), Adobe, Figma, TikTok Symphony survey ("76% likely to increase use"), Meshy "a minute and a dollar", DreamShop e-commerce testimonials. The report should mark each with "(vendor survey)".

## 7. Guidance for the report writer

### 7.1 Conclusions that are robust (multiple independent sources or verified)

1. **Adoption in game development is broad at the company level and shallow at the worker level, and the worker level is growing slowly.** Company/studio "any use" is saturated (52% in an independent survey by 2025; 90–96% in vendor surveys). Individual use is 31% (2024) → 36% (2026), only 30% inside studios, and concentrated in research/brainstorming, admin and code assistance rather than final assets. This is the cleanest contrast with coding, where individual-developer adoption climbed far faster in the same window.
2. **Sentiment moved the opposite way from adoption:** negative views 18% → 30% → 52% (2024–2026), highest in the crafts most exposed to substitution (art 64%, design/narrative 63%) and still 59% among programmers. Required caveat: GDC measures "impact on the industry", not personal satisfaction.
3. **Player-facing gen AI ships mostly from small, new entrants.** ~20% of 2025 and ~31% of 2026-YTD Steam releases carry a disclosure; ~60% of disclosures are visual assets; most disclosing publishers are one-off; AI-flagged titles account for most of the growth in release counts. Required caveats: self-reported; excludes coding tools since Jan 2026; share of releases ≠ share of catalog ≠ share of revenue.
4. **Supply is expanding far faster than demand wherever uploading is cheap** (Steam releases ~2× 2020 with half of 2025 releases getting <10 reviews; Deezer >50% of uploads but 1–3% of streams; Adobe Stock ~48% of inventory; Douyin AI-drama uploads). This is the strongest support for the user's "volume ≠ value" thesis and holds even if any single figure is off.
5. **Substitution shows up first in commodity execution layers** — outsourced 2D illustration and concept art (China 2023 price collapse), translation/localization (freelance postings −20–50%; China MT/MTPE >66% per TAC), UA/ad creative (Meta 4M+ advertisers), incidental VO (ARC Raiders TTS) — and in juniors/novices (Teutloff; CVL; TAC), not in hero content. Required caveat: the causal decomposition (AI vs. post-2021 China licensing freeze, post-pandemic contraction, capital-cost reset) is not measured anywhere.
6. **Western institutions gate premium content**: SAG-AFTRA IMA consent/disclosure/replica minimums, Equity's ballot, platform disclosure rules, audience backlash (ARC Raiders, Black Ops 7, Amazon dubs, Coca-Cola). China lacks equivalent gates and moved earlier and deeper in art, dubbing and short drama.
7. **Frontier tech (world models) is research/previs-grade in games** (Muse/WHAMM limits verified; Genie session limits per analysts; Decart/General Intuition revenue outside games) even as capital floods in.

### 7.2 Conclusions that are fragile (use with the caveats above, or hedge)

1. **Any magnitude of efficiency gain** (50% concept art, 3× modeling, +300%, 70% QA target). None is audited or project-level; present as "claimed" and note that no controlled study of game-production productivity exists.
2. **Any "AI share of work/assets" figure** (37 Games >80%; "art-stage >80%"). Definitions unknown.
3. **The 2027–28 "half of Steam releases" projection.** Author's extrapolation across a policy change.
4. **AI-attributed layoffs.** Only King is even reported as AI-driven, from anonymous sources. The 2023–24 layoff peak pre-dates production-scale gen AI; say "AI's labor effect is visible in outsourcing prices and junior demand, not in attributed layoffs".
5. **Cost-structure arithmetic** ($50–95M art spend; art 25–30%). Vendor rules of thumb with mixed denominators — illustrative at best.
6. **Rec Room as the "AI unit-economics" cautionary tale.** The AI-cost detail is unconfirmed.
7. **China 2026 short-drama "majority AI"** and "98.7% lose money". Platform-specific upload ratios and a headline.
8. **Newzoo +6.1%** and the "$16B Steam" figure. Methodology/estimate issues.
9. **"Roblox bookings guided down despite AI tools"** — true as reported, but the causal reading (AI does not fix demand concentration) is inference against a viral-hit comparison base.
10. **The tier model in N3 §8 and the discipline table in N1 §8** are the researchers' judgments; present them as frameworks, with the evidence-type tags carried over.

### 7.3 On the user's two methodological perspectives

- **(a) Games ≠ coding; value comes from creative work and polish.** The evidence supports the direction: measured/claimed AI penetration is deepest in verifiable, commodity, execution tasks (code assistance, localization, UA creatives, placeholder art/VO, QA) and thinnest in hero content and "the last 20%" (ARC Raiders re-recording; Black Ops 7 backlash; Joseph Kim's essay is title-level only). But the folder has **no time-use data** splitting creative vs execution vs decision/iteration hours for any game or creative discipline (N1 §6 gaps, N3 §6 gaps). The report should say so explicitly and derive the shift qualitatively: AI raised execution capacity → output volume rose in open markets → human hours moved toward specification, selection, validation and repair (GDC task list; Figma/Adobe vendor surveys; MTPE and paintover pricing) — and that validation layer is priced at a discount. Do not present percentages for the three-way split; none exist.
- **(b) Volume ≠ value; no incremental industry-level value yet.** Supported for games as strongly as the data allow: global market growth (+~6%, Newzoo; China +7.7% in 2025, +12% in H1 2026) is attributed by the sources to spending per player and specific hits, never to AI; AI-disclosed titles' cumulative estimated gross (~$0.66B) is a small fraction of Steam's annual estimated gross; half of releases get almost no attention; AI-native standalone games show attention without conversion. Required framing: this is *absence of evidence* of incremental value plus positive evidence of supply inflation — not proof that AI destroyed value. Also note the counter-evidence the notes under-weight: Chinese majors report record games revenue and rising R&D while claiming AI pipelines (correlation only), and creator payouts on Roblox/Fortnite are rising (but pre-date gen-AI creation tools).
