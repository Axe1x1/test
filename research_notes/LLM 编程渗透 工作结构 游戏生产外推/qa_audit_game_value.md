## 1. Overall verdict

**Reliability of the note: high on its headline numbers, medium on its inferences, low-medium on its historical argument.** Of 60 claims checked, **25 verified or partly verified against source extracts (8 of them with a correction to a value, date or definition), 1 further corrected (Gamalytic denominator), 1 disputed and not to be used (2026 YTD count), and 33 unverifiable this session (27 internally consistent and usable with caveat; 6 weak or not to be used)**. Beyond the table, three of the note's inferences and two source attributions are corrected below. The note's core quantitative spine — Steam release counts, the "half of releases get <10 reviews" finding, the WeLoveIt/VaporLens concentration study, Alinea's H1-2026 back-catalog share, Newzoo's 2025/2026 market and playtime figures, the China 2025 official figures, the three "AI-disclosed games perform worse" studies, the Quantic Foundry/Circana surveys, Roblox DevEx concentration, Fortnite's creator-island share, AppsFlyer's UA figures, Bain's 83%-vs-50% claim — **all check out as reported**. The synthesis is therefore built on real numbers.

The problems are in what the note does with them:

1. **Three inferences contradict the note's own (verified) sources.** (a) "New releases' share of play time is falling" — Newzoo says the share is *constant at ~13%* and improved modestly since 2021; on PC, non-annual new releases rose from 4% to 10% of playtime and from 16% to 30% of PC revenue (2022–25). The falling series is Steam *revenue* share of new releases (29%→21%, H1 2024→H1 2026), a different metric. (b) "Extra volume adds to the long tail, not the top" — Newzoo 2026: PC spending outside the top 20 rose from 48% to 56% (2022–25); the *mid-head* (ranks ~21–1,000) widened. (c) "AI raises acquisition costs" — AppsFlyer's own numbers (spend +3.8%, impressions +20%) imply a *lower* cost per impression; "cost of standing out" is AppsFlyer's qualitative framing, not a measured CPI rise.
2. **Two source conflations.** The "$290 median for 2026 releases" is from Games-Stats.com (Q1-2026 cohort, Jan 1–Apr 22), not from the Game World Observer article; and the GWO article is itself a re-report of the WeLoveIt dataset, so "84.5%" and "84.6%" are one source, not two.
3. **One bad number in the supply table.** The 2026 year-to-date count of 20,253 (pace 27–28k) is inconsistent with SteamDB's H1 count (it would require ~101 releases/day in Q3 vs 66/day in H1) and must not be used.
4. **The "AI-disclosed games perform worse" evidence is over-read as a demand penalty.** All three studies use review counts as the outcome *or* as the sales proxy (they are not independent), none matches on budget or quality, and "AI-flagged" includes ARC Raiders-type hits that used AI for minor voice lines. The causal claim should be stated as "a measurable *reception* gap consistent with stigma plus selection", with the 53% figure reported on its actual base (4 vs 7 first-month reviews).
5. **The historical-analog section is one-sided.** It selects four episodes that end in "median collapse" and omits that the same cheap-production tiers (mods, UGC, indies, mobile F2P) produced the largest genre innovations and audience expansions of the last 15 years, and that Steam's own gross grew ~3–4× during the flood. The verified drivers of Steam's 2026 record (new Chinese players, higher prices, viral co-op hits) also contradict the "fixed attention pool" premise for PC.
6. **Confidence labels in §6 are one notch too high** on points 2–4 (median decline, concentration, demand-bound revenue) and the upside channels (mid-head growth, new genres, localization-driven market reach, live-ops survival economics) are missing from the formulation. A balanced formulation is given in §6 below.

None of this overturns the user's "volume ≠ value" thesis; it sharpens it. The right statement is: *cheap production is necessary but not sufficient for value creation; where it arrived together with a new distribution channel, business model or genre it expanded the market; where it arrived alone it diluted the median.* AI so far is arriving alone.

## 3. Definition, denominator and causality warnings

1. **Releases ≠ store pages ≠ catalog.** Haro's shares are of *releases in a period*; Totally Human's 7,818/10,258 are *store pages carrying a disclosure at a date* (incl. unreleased and back-catalog pages); WeLoveIt's 99,206 is *paid games with ≥1 review*. (Aligned with QA-C warning 5.)
2. **Gross vs net, and what is excluded.** Gamalytic and WeLoveIt report *estimated gross before Valve's ~30%*, modelled from reviews (× 20–60) and CCU; Gamalytic excludes DLC/IAP; WeLoveIt excludes F2P. Excluding F2P removes CS2/Dota 2/etc. from the "top 1% = 84.5%" population — on an all-revenue basis concentration would be *higher*, but the population is different. Alinea and GameDiscoverCo are different estimation series (≈$20B vs $16.9B for 2025); never mix them.
3. **Units vs revenue vs players.** "Copies" (Steam sales) ≠ "players" (Game Pass/mobile installs): Palworld's 25M were 15M copies + 10M Xbox players; Vampire Survivors' 27M are players. Haro's "sales share" is a review-count proxy for *units*, not revenue.
4. **Right-censoring in cohort medians.** "Median declining for 12 consecutive years", "$1,728 for 2026 releases" and "$290 for Q1-2026 releases" all compare cohorts of different ages at one date. Newer cohorts *must* look worse. The defensible statement: "the median lifetime estimate for paid Steam games is ≈$3.6k, and cohort medians measured at the same age have fallen since the mid-2010s (direction robust; magnitude not)".
5. **Composition effect in "the median".** Steam Direct (2017) and now AI changed *who publishes*: the median of all uploads includes hobby projects that would never have been published before. The report must not infer "a competent, marketed studio game earns less every year" from the all-uploads median; that would need a series conditioned on marketing/wishlists (GameDiscoverCo-style), which no note has.
6. **Opt-in vs panel surveys, and different questions.** Quantic Foundry (opt-in, young core/hardcore, 85% negative *attitude*) ≠ Circana (panel, 25% *purchase intent* penalty) ≠ GameDiscoverCo (self-selected engaged Steam users, 8% refuse) ≠ Bain (5,300 gamers, "comfort" trend). They are consistent once the question and sample are named: an intense minority (~8% refuse; ~25% less likely) and an indifferent majority, most negative among engaged PC players. Do not average them.
7. **"AI-disclosed games perform worse" is reception + selection, not a measured demand penalty.** (a) Selection: low-budget, no-marketing projects are both more likely to use (and disclose) AI art and more likely to fail; controls for genre/timing/experience do not remove this. (b) Reverse causality: weak projects reach for AI assets. (c) Outcome circularity: Game Oracle's outcome is review *count* (4 vs 7 first-month reviews); Haro's *sales* share is estimated *from* review counts; Bazzaz & Cooper's outcome is review *sentiment* vs a high-rated PCG baseline — three views of the same signal, not three independent measurements of revenue. (d) Heterogeneity: Burton himself says the penalty is concentrated in "high-potential" games; the median game in both groups fails regardless. (e) "AI-flagged" ≠ "AI-built": the Q4-2025 27% sales share is ARC Raiders using TTS for minor lines. State it as: "disclosed generative AI carries a measurable *reception* discount among engaged PC players (fewer reviews, ~3–4 pts lower positivity, ~18 pts lower recommendation vs PCG games), part of which is selection; the effect on revenue is not cleanly identified."
8. **Playtime share vs revenue share of new releases move in opposite directions.** New releases: ~12–13% of PC+console playtime (stable/slightly up since 2021; PC non-annual releases up sharply) but 29%→21% of Steam H1 revenue (2024→2026). Both are true; the note must not cite the second as evidence for a falling first.
9. **Concentration depends on the cut point.** "Top 1% ≈ 85%" is ~1,000 titles out of ~100k paid games; the *top 20* share of PC spending fell 52%→44% (2022–25). The head is ~1,000 games wide and the mid-head is growing — this is where new-entrant value has actually shown up.
10. **Series discontinuities.** Newzoo re-based in Q1 2025 and its 2025 actual added D2C/mini-game coverage; Sensor Tower is app-store-only (misses web shops and mini-games); Circana is US-only; Newzoo playtime is quarterly vs a Q1-2021 lockdown peak. No clean 2015–2026 global hours series exists (the note says so; keep saying so).
11. **Payout growth vs rate changes.** Roblox's +50% DevEx growth includes a 42% increase in the qualifying DevEx rate (Apr 2026); Fortnite's +11% is on a like-for-like Mar–Dec basis.
12. **Demand is not fixed.** Steam H1 2026 +14.5% (new Chinese players, price rises), PC playtime +3%, PC revenue +12% (Newzoo 2025), China H1 2026 +12.2%, global +9.1%/+6.1%. "Fixed attention pool" is true for MM8 console, false for PC and China. Say "slow-growing attention in mature console markets; growing on PC and in China/emerging markets".
13. **"AI-enabled surge" is descriptive.** AI-flagged games are 60–90% of the *growth* in monthly releases (2023→2026), but the same window includes cheaper engines (Godot/Unity), post-layoff solo shipping, and disclosure-rule effects; the counterfactual release count without AI is unknown. Write "coincides with"/"is concentrated in AI-flagged titles", not "is caused by".
14. **Author-estimated ratios.** Genshin content cost "10–20% of revenue" (better: 5–20%), Voodoo "0.4%", Roblox top-1,000 "82%" are derived/estimated; label them.

## 4. Assessment of the historical-analog argument

**What is supported (descriptively, by verified data):** each cheap-production episode — open Steam (2015→), hyper-casual (2018–22), UGC platforms, WeChat mini-games (2023→) — shows (1) a surge in titles, (2) a collapse in the *median*, (3) persistent concentration at the top, and (4) the bottleneck moving to discovery/UA. This is enough to support the user's "volume ≠ value" caution and the prediction that AI-driven volume mostly lengthens the tail.

**What is overstated:**
- "Total market value followed demand and platform traffic, not supply" and "no case where a production-cost shock by itself enlarged total player spending" are untestable as stated ("by itself") and contradicted in spirit by the note's own evidence: Steam's gross grew roughly **3–4×** (≈$4–5B in 2017 by SteamSpy-era estimates → ≈$17B in 2025) while releases grew ~3×; the mid-head widened (PC spend outside top 20 48%→56%; non-annual new releases 16%→30% of PC revenue).
- The hyper-casual bust was proximately a **distribution/privacy shock** (Apple ATT) plus post-COVID normalization, not evidence that cheap production destroys value; hyper-casual still delivered 4–7B downloads a year and seeded the hybrid-casual category ($20–100M/yr hits at Voodoo).
- UGC "reallocation" is asserted from one Newzoo PC-time statistic; Roblox's growth (DAU 17.6M in 2019 → 151.5M in Q3 2025; 39.6B hours/quarter) is overwhelmingly new, young, mobile-first players, not PC hours moved from other games.

**Counter-evidence the note omits and the report should include:**
1. **Mobile F2P (2008–15)** — the largest audience and revenue expansion in the industry's history (mobile ≈$0 in 2007 → $113B in 2025) arrived as a *bundle*: new distribution (app stores), new business model (F2P), and cheap production. Cost shock and market expansion co-occurred; the note treats mobile only through the hyper-casual bust (selection on the negative case).
2. **The cheapest production tier created the biggest genres of the last 15 years:** MOBA (WarCraft III mod → LoL, Dota 2), battle royale (ARMA/H1Z1 mods → PUBG → Fortnite BR), auto-battlers (Dota 2 mod), survival-crafting (Minecraft, one developer, the best-selling game ever), roguelike deck-builders (Slay the Spire → Balatro), "Vampire Survivors-likes", extraction shooters, and the 2023–25 "friendslop" co-op wave (Lethal Company, Content Warning, R.E.P.O., PEAK, Chained Together) that Alinea names as a 2026 revenue driver. The note lists Vampire Survivors "spawning a genre" but does not draw the conclusion: cheap experimentation is how the market finds new demand.
3. **New players via reach:** Alinea's H1-2026 growth came partly from an influx of Chinese players; localization and language reach expand markets, and AI localization/voice are exactly the "invisible" uses over-represented among *successful* AI-flagged games (Haro).
4. **Live-ops survival economics:** lower content cost can keep mid-tier live games alive (fewer Concord/2025-style shutdowns), which shows up as retained spending rather than new spending — unmeasured but plausible, and the opposite of the note's "Red Queen only" reading.
5. **Ball's own framing** is "record revenues alongside stagnant *participation* in MM8", i.e. growth exists (China, PC, price, older players) — the pie is not fixed, it is shifting.

**Correct reading for the report:** cheap production is **necessary but not sufficient**. When it arrives *with* a new channel, business model or genre, it has expanded the market (mobile, UGC platforms, mod-born genres, co-op indies). When it arrives *alone*, it dilutes the median and moves rents to platforms and discovery. Generative AI so far is arriving alone (no new channel or business model; visible AI even carries a reception discount) — which is why the 2024–26 data look like dilution. The open question is whether an AI-native channel/model appears (§6, point 10).

## 6. Recommended balanced synthesis (for the report writer)

Use this formulation (Chinese wording is the writer's; the logic and hedges are what matter):

1. **Supply.** Steam releases rose ≈7× from 2015 (2.8k) to 2025 (≈19.5–20k) and are on pace for >24k in 2026 (SteamDB, H1 basis). Since 2024 the *growth* in monthly releases is 60–90% accounted for by AI-flagged titles (Haro), which were 11%→20%→31% of releases (2024→2026 YTD; definition narrowed in Jan 2026). Similar dynamics in WeChat mini-games (500k+ developers) and UGC platforms.
2. **Demand.** Attention in mature console markets is flat to down (MM8 software −8% 2021–24; PC+console playtime −1% in 2025 vs a 2021 lockdown peak), but **demand is growing** on PC (+3% playtime, +12% revenue), in China (+7.7% 2025, +12.2% H1 2026) and through pricing/D2C (Newzoo 2025 +9.1%, partly better coverage; Steam H1 2026 +14.5% on a Chinese-player influx and higher prices). So: **slow-growing, shifting demand — not a fixed pool.**
3. **Distribution of outcomes.** Half of releases get <10 reviews; ~two-thirds of a 2025 cohort grossed <$1k (Gamalytic, Jan–Oct cohort); the median paid Steam game's lifetime estimate is ≈$3.6k; the top 1% (~1,000 titles) take ≈85% of estimated paid-game revenue (WeLoveIt/VaporLens). **But** the head is ~1,000 titles wide and widening: PC spend outside the top 20 rose 48%→56% and non-annual new releases rose 16%→30% of PC revenue (2022–25). The opportunity for new entrants is the *mid-head*, not the top 20 and not the tail.
4. **AI and value so far.** AI-flagged titles are a third of releases but 10–27% of estimated units (hit-driven; 17% in Q1 2026); disclosed AI carries a *reception* discount among engaged PC players (fewer reviews, ~3–4 pts lower positivity, ~18 pts lower recommendation vs PCG games), part of which is selection; successful AI-flagged games use AI where players don't look (voice, localization), failures use it for visuals (72%). No source attributes any industry growth to AI (absence of evidence, not evidence of destruction). AI-native consumer products are small in games terms (app-store companions ≈$120M in 2025, a floor) but consumer AI already competes for leisure time.
5. **What history says.** Cheap production alone dilutes (Steam 2015→, hyper-casual, mini-games); cheap production *plus* a new channel/model/genre expands the market (mobile F2P, mod-born genres, UGC platforms, co-op indies). Generative AI is currently the former; the swing factor is whether it becomes the latter (AI-native genres, personalization, creation-as-play on UGC platforms).
6. **Where value will be created (2026–2030).** (a) Invisible cost/margin gains (code, QA, localization, tools, secondary VO, prototyping) — partly competed away, retained by platforms, IP holders and mid-head studios with taste; (b) faster search for fun (more prototypes per hit) — the same mechanism that produced past genre breakouts, so both a redistribution *and* the main upside; (c) reach (localization/voice into new languages) — the one AI use with a demonstrated positive association; (d) live-ops survival for mid-tier games — plausible, unmeasured; (e) AI-native or personalized experiences — small, unmeasurable once embedded, the key long-run uncertainty. **Risks:** visible-AI stigma in premium PC/console; template-segment homogenization (mini-games, "-likes"); attention competition in UA (more impressions, not shown to be pricier).
7. **Bottom line vs software.** In coding, cheaper production converts into more delivered function because demand is productive and latent; in games, it converts mainly into more attempts at a slow-growing, hit-driven attention budget. The conversion ratio is lower, not zero: history shows the pie grows when cheap production meets a new way to reach or engage players, and that is the bet AI-in-games has yet to make.
