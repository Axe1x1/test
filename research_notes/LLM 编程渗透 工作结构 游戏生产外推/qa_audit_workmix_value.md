# QA Audit — Work-composition shift (C/E/D) and "volume ≠ value" notes

**Scope.** Independent fact-check and reasoning audit of two notes in this folder:
- `software_work_composition_shift.md` (below: **W**) — pre-AI time allocation, task-mix change, output volume, bottleneck shift, roles/labor market, frameworks, synthesized C/E/D split.
- `ai_coding_value_evidence.md` (below: **V**) — macro/industry statistics, firm ROI surveys, software-industry revenue/structure, value capture, mechanisms, counter-evidence, verdict on "volume ≠ value".

`coding_adoption_penetration.md` (**A**) and `ai_code_share_productivity.md` (**S**) were read in full for cross-note consistency only (their audit is another reviewer's job; `qa_audit_coding_adoption_share.md` appeared while this audit was being written and is reconciled in §9 — several rows below were amended accordingly). `qa_audit_game_creative.md` was read for format alignment; it shares no quantitative coding facts with W/V, so no conflicts arise.

**Date.** 2026-09-28.

**Method.** (1) Both notes read in full; ~80 quantitative claims extracted, the ~60 most load-bearing tabulated below. (2) Primary pages fetched directly where the proxy allows: anthropic.com (12 research/news pages), claude.com (1), microsoft.com (Meyer et al. TSE preprint PDF — text extracted locally; Cui et al. page), cloud.google.com (DORA 2024 and 2025 announcement posts), github.com (Innovation Graph and microsoft/working-with-ai repos cloned; CSVs recomputed). (3) WebSearch **did work in this session** (~60 queries; the shared budget was evidently not exhausted for this reviewer). Search-result summaries were used to confirm secondary-only figures; they are marked "V (search)" and are weaker than a direct primary read. (4) Definition, level-of-analysis and arithmetic checks.

**Verification limits.** Status key aligned with `qa_audit_game_creative.md`: **V** verified (primary read or ≥2 concordant search results quoting the primary) · **V (search)** verified via search-result summaries only · **VP** partially verified · **C** corrected · **D** disputed · **U** unverifiable, with sub-tags **[U-consistent]** (use "as reported" with the caveat), **[U-weak]** (single/low-quality source; caveat or omit), **[U-no]** (do not use). Nothing was "corrected" from memory alone; where my background knowledge disagrees with a note, the row says so and the item goes to the VERIFICATION QUEUE.

## 1. Overall verdict per note

**W — software_work_composition_shift.md.** Reliability: **high on the evidence rows, medium-low on the synthesized split.** Every anthropic.com figure the note relies on (AEI Apr/Sep 2025, Jan/Mar/Jun 2026; internal study Dec 2025; Claude Code expertise Jun 2026; 81k survey; skills RCT; labor-market paper) checks out verbatim; the Meyer et al. Table 2 baseline is confirmed from the primary PDF; the GitHub Innovation Graph table reproduces exactly from the raw CSVs (2022–2026Q1 sums, YoY, developer stocks, pushes per account); the MSR applicability scores match the repository CSV to three decimals; Hoffmann, Cui, Faros 2025, DORA 2024/2025, Stack Overflow 2025, JetBrains 2025, Atlassian 2025, GitClear, CodeRabbit, Veracode, Canaries, Indeed, Challenger, SignalFire, Hosseini & Lichtinger, Karpathy and Vogels all confirmed by search. Main weaknesses: (a) the three-way split table (§8) is presented with more precision than its inputs allow — the 2026 columns rest on shares of *AI sessions* and *decisions inside sessions*, self-forecasts and frontier anecdotes, not on any human time-use measurement, and the note's own ±10–15-point caveat is buried in "Gaps"; (b) the Meyer-to-C/E/D mapping puts meetings, helping and email (34% of the day) entirely outside the split although the paper itself says they are "common and constructive" during planning/specification/release, which understates C and D in the baseline; (c) the "Phase 1: human E share rose" claim rests on one study (Hoffmann) that measures shares of GitHub *activities* among open-source developers, not time, so the "U-shape" is a hypothesis with one observation on each side; (d) "value per unit fell at the margin … total value rose (session value +27%)" misreads Anthropic's +27%, which is a *per-session* (per-unit) value estimate and therefore cuts the other way; (e) it missed two 2026 updates that strengthen its own bottleneck thesis — Faros "AI Acceleration Whiplash" (Jun 2026: median review time +441%, unreviewed merges +31%) and DORA's 2026 ROI report ("verification tax", J-curve) — and the Sep 2026 Anthropic CI post (Claude authors 80% of code; 8× code shipped; 25× CI jobs).

**V — ai_coding_value_evidence.md.** Reliability: **high on the survey/macro layer, medium on company revenue figures, medium-high on the verdict.** The macro and firm-survey anchors (BLS Q2 2026 and 2025 productivity, BLS software publishers +12.9% with 2024 revised 9.4%→4.0%, SF Fed Letter 2026-14, St. Louis Fed earnings-call study, Goldman Feb/Mar 2026, NBER w34836, w34984, Humlum & Vestergaard, PwC, McKinsey, BCG, MIT NANDA, Bain), the industry layer (Octoverse 2025, Appfigures, Sensor Tower, Stripe letter, TCS FY26, Indian IT "AI deflation", SaaSpocalypse) and the value-capture layer (Anthropic $14B/$30B, Claude Code $2.5B, Cursor–SpaceX $60B, consumer surplus $97B/$116→172B, Indeed, Canaries) all confirmed. The graded, level-tagged verdict is the best-argued section in the folder. Main weaknesses: (a) a handful of misattributions/conflations — the 0.07% TFP figure belongs to the St. Louis Fed July 2026 piece; "64% automation" for API coding is the *directive* share of all API traffic; "90 minutes" and "1.4 hours" are the same number, not overall-vs-software; the "$2.6B ≈ 75% enterprise" Cursor line conflates Reuters' total-revenue figure with Forbes' $4B; Lovable's "$500M with 146 staff (Mar 2026)" merges a Mar 2026 ($400M) and a Jun 2026 ($500M) datapoint; NBER w34836's abstract says ">80%" report no impact, not 89–90%; (b) the "app supply +60–104% (2026) vs spending +10.6% (2025)" comparison is a period mismatch; (c) the software-publishers signal needs one more caveat that matters for this project: NAICS 5132 includes video-game publishers, so the +12.9% partly reflects the games business the user is asking about; (d) "Confidence: High that the [vendor] revenue is real" is generous for unaudited run-rates; (e) the counter-evidence section omits the strongest *independent* value estimate in the folder (Daniotti et al., $9.6–14.4B/yr US, from note S), the Stanford/Denisov-Blanch 15–20% private-repo estimate, and a macro fact from the very BLS release it cites — labor share at 52.8%, the lowest since 1947 — which bears directly on *who* captures gains.

## 3. Definition, level-of-analysis and causality warnings

1. **Time vs activity vs sessions vs decisions.** The C/E/D question is about human *time*. Only Meyer et al. (2019), IDC, Software.com and Stripe measure time. Hoffmann measures *GitHub activity shares*; AEI and the Claude Code papers measure *shares of AI conversations/sessions* and *shares of decisions inside sessions*; Anthropic's internal study measures *shares of Claude Code tasks* plus self-report. W's §8 table silently converts all of these into "share of engineering time". The report must say which unit each row is in.
2. **"Share of code" ≠ "share of work" ≠ "share of value".** Google 75% and Anthropic 80% are undefined authorship metrics (accepted suggestions, lines, or PRs); CodeRabbit/GitClear show that AI code is more verbose and more duplicated, which mechanically inflates any line-based share. Note S already makes this point; W §8's "output composition" table should carry it.
3. **"Mass-produced" conflates two things:** machine-produced (measurable: Google, Anthropic, Faros PR counts, Innovation Graph) and routine/low-value (not measured). The only direct per-unit value signal in the folder — Anthropic's model-estimated average session value **+27%** — points *up*, not down; quality proxies (duplication, issues per PR, churn, incidents) point down but measure defects, not value. W §3's inference "more units, lower average value per unit, higher total value" is a hypothesis; the "+27%" must not be used as evidence for "total value rose".
4. **Level of analysis in the "volume ≠ value" verdict.** TASK (RCTs: +26%; −19% for experts) → TEAM (Faros/DORA: throughput up, review and instability up) → FIRM (surveys: most see no P&L effect; implementers ~30% in coding) → INDUSTRY (software publishers +12.9% LP; SaaS growth slowing; IT services deflation; app supply ≫ app spend) → MACRO (LP 2.1–2.2%; TFP ~0). V keeps these apart well; the report should keep the tags.
5. **"Industry" needs a definition before the hypothesis can be scored.** (a) incumbent software producers' profit/value added: no visible AI-coding gain (high confidence); (b) the software industry including AI labs and coding-tool vendors: tens of billions of new (unaudited, low-margin) revenue (medium-high); (c) social value (consumer/user surplus, cheaper IT services for buyers): large but unpriced (medium). The user's hypothesis is true for (a), false for (b), and beside the point for (c).
6. **Self-report vs telemetry vs administrative data.** METR's perception gap (believed −20%, measured +19%) discredits *self-reported* gains specifically; DORA's ">80% perceive gains", Anthropic's "+50%", Atlassian's "68% save >10 h" and all CEO surveys are self-report. Telemetry (Faros, GitClear, Innovation Graph) and administrative data (ADP, Danish registers, BLS) should be weighted higher and labelled.
7. **Correlation vs causation.** Faros (cross-sectional and within-org before/after), DORA (cross-sectional models), Indeed ("since Claude Code's release"), BCG/McKinsey "high performers", Klarna/Shopify/Salesforce headcount cases (post-2021 over-hiring correction, rates, Section 174, guidance misses) are all correlational or confounded. Only Cui et al., METR, Peng et al. and the Anthropic skills RCT are causal at task level; Canaries, Hosseini & Lichtinger and Humlum & Vestergaard are quasi-causal at labor-market level.
8. **Vendor provenance.** Most bottleneck/quality evidence comes from firms selling analytics or review tools (Faros, GitClear, CodeRabbit, Veracode, DX) and most usage evidence from AI labs (Anthropic, GitHub/Microsoft, Google). The report should say this once, then note that non-vendor sources (DORA 2026, METR, Song et al., Anthropic's own CI post) point the same way on review/verification load.
9. **Period mismatches.** App releases (Q1–Apr 2026) vs app spend (calendar 2025); Innovation Graph (calendar year, bot-filtered) vs Octoverse (Sep–Aug, all accounts); Google's share series (Oct 2024 call, "fall 2025", Cloud Next Apr 2026) vs Anthropic (Sep 2026); Canaries (to Jun 2026) vs Indeed (to May 2026). Always date the comparison.
10. **Percent vs percentage points.** DORA "+14%" = 14 pp; Anthropic skills RCT "17% lower" = 17 pp; Hoffmann "+12.4%" = +5.4 pp. Chinese text should use 百分点 where appropriate.

## 4. Assessment of the three-way work-split estimate (W §8)

**What is defensible.** The pre-AI anchor is real (Meyer Table 2, verified). Direction of change in the agentic era is well supported: humans keep ~70% of planning decisions and make only ~20% of execution decisions inside Claude Code sessions; "fixing broken code" sessions fell from 33% to 19% while operating/writing/analysis grew; Anthropic engineers report less time per task with much more output; review load and verification are now the documented constraint (Faros 2025/2026, DORA 2026, Vogels, Anthropic CI 25×); junior (execution-heavy) hiring fell while senior/AI-titled postings rebounded. The claim "the share of *machine-produced* output rose" is verified on every available volume metric.

**What is not defensible as stated.**
1. *The category mapping.* Meyer's "Coding" includes reading code (a D-type activity) and writing tests (E); "Bugfixing" is split 50/50 by assumption; and 34% of the workday (meetings 15%, email 10%, interruptions 4%, helping 5%) is parked outside the split even though the paper says those activities are constructive during planning, specification and release — i.e., they carry much of the real C and D. Restricting the denominator to "development-heavy activities" therefore overstates the pre-AI E share. Two baselines should be shown: narrow (as W does): E ≈55–60% / D ≈30–35% / C ≈8–12%; broad (allocating half of meetings/helping/interruptions to C/D): E ≈40–45% / D ≈35–40% / C ≈15–25%. Neither measures "creative" work; the paper has no such category.
2. *The 2023–24 column ("E share rose").* One study (Hoffmann), measuring activity shares of open-source developers under completion-era Copilot (data from the 2022–23 rollout), plus RCT throughput gains. Plausible and worth reporting, but as "activity-share evidence consistent with a temporary rise in hands-on coding", not as a measured time shift. The "U-shape" is a hypothesis with one observation on each side.
3. *The mid-2026 median column (E 40–50 / D 40–45 / C 10–15).* No input measures human time in 2026. The inputs are AI-session shares (Claude users), decisions-within-sessions, one engineer's self-forecast ("70%+ reviewer"), Karpathy's "99%", and adoption surveys (90% use AI; ~2 h/day median; 14% used agents daily in mid-2025; JetBrains' "68% daily agents" in mid-2026 is definition-sensitive). The column is an informed guess and should be labelled as such, with ±15 points. Two mid-2026 datapoints surfaced by the sibling audit narrow it somewhat and should be cited [S]: JetBrains (May–Jul 2026, >15k devs): **more than half of developers now write less than 20% of their code by hand**, ~22% have >80% agent-generated code (East Asia 32–35%), 23% remain "manual coders"; Stack Overflow pulse (May 2026): 59% use agents at work at any frequency (31% in 2025), 40% of developers daily. These are self-reported shares of *code*, not time; they support a hands-on E share of *output* well below half for the median developer, while the time share stays unmeasured because review and steering time is not captured.
4. *The AI-intensive column (E 15–25 / D 55–65 / C 15–20).* Better supported (70/20 decisions; 80% authored; 8× shipped; "not writing code 99% of the time"), but it describes Anthropic, Google, and startups on Claude Code, and part of the E→D move is **relabeling**: specifying and steering an agent is execution by other means. State the operational rule used ("hands-on writing = E; instructing/reviewing agents = D") because moving that boundary shifts 10–20 points between E and D.
5. *"Iteration" being automated.* The 33%→19% fall in fix-sessions is a composition change in a fast-growing, broadening user base (operating/writing/analysis sessions grew), not a measurement of less human debugging per developer. Keep the point, soften the causal wording.
6. *Value per unit.* See §3.3 above: the note's only per-unit value datapoint (+27% per session) contradicts its own "value per unit fell" inference; the inference should be reframed as "quality per unit fell on vendor proxies; value per unit unmeasured".

**Recommended version for the report (present as an *estimate*, not data; 置信度 as shown):**

| Category (human engineering time; hands-on rule as stated above) | Pre-AI (2019 Microsoft time diary; dev-heavy activities only) | 2023–24 completion/chat era | Median developer, mid-2026 | AI-intensive / agent-first teams, mid-2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **E 执行/量产** (hands-on writing of code, tests, docs, routine fixes) | 55–60% (broad baseline: 40–45%) | flat to slightly ↑ (activity-share evidence, OSS only) | 35–50% | 10–25% |
| **D 决策/验证/迭代** (review, verification, debugging oversight, spec refinement, prioritizing, steering agents) | 30–35% (broad: 35–40%) | ≈ flat | 35–50% | 50–65% |
| **C 创造/设计** (framing, ideation, architecture, novel solutions; explicit only) | 8–12% (+ unmeasured design inside "coding") | flat / slightly ↑ (more exploration) | 10–20% | 15–25% |
| Confidence | medium (single firm, self-report, 2019) | low (direction only) | **very low** (no time-use measurement exists; ±15 pts) | low-medium on levels, **high on direction** |

Required caveats in the report: (i) no post-2024 time-use study exists — this is the single biggest gap; (ii) the 2026 columns are triangulated from AI-session shares, decision shares, self-reports and frontier anecdotes; (iii) the E→D shift is partly a relabeling of the same cognitive work; (iv) the frontier column over-represents web/front-end, greenfield and startup work (JS/TS + HTML/CSS = 59% of Claude coding queries; startups 33% of Claude Code conversations) and under-represents legacy/expert contexts (METR) and ops/deployment (76% don't plan to use AI there).

**"Why did the shares change" — evidence-backed drivers to use:** (1) execution cost collapsed with agents (pushes +34%→+80%; PRs 4.7×; Google 75%; Anthropic 80%/8×); (2) verification capacity did not scale (Faros review time +91% → +441%; unreviewed merges +31%; DORA "verification tax"; CI 25×); (3) humans retain planning/acceptance decisions (70/20); (4) capacity created *new* work (27% net-new; 48% cite scope expansion) rather than only speeding old work; (5) firms substituted junior execution capacity (Canaries, Hosseini & Lichtinger, SignalFire) and hired for senior/AI roles (Indeed). Drivers (4)–(5) are what "mass-production share rose because capacity rose" looks like in the data.

## 5. Assessment of the "volume ≠ value" verdict (V §8)

**Is the evidence balanced?** Largely yes. Both sides are represented at each level, the strongest counterexamples (BLS +12.9%, vendor revenue, Stripe cohorts, Base44, Amazon migration, consumer surplus, posting rebound) are included, and the graded conclusions are honest about confidence. Adjustments:

1. **Upgrade the review/verification mechanism from "strong" to "strong and multi-sourced"**: Faros 2025 (+91%), Faros 2026 (+441% median review time; +31% unreviewed merges; incidents/PR +243%), DORA 2026 verification tax, Song et al. (+8% coordination time), Anthropic CI (25× jobs; "writing code is no longer the constraint"). This is now the best-evidenced mechanism in the folder.
2. **Add missing counter-evidence:** Daniotti et al. (arXiv 2506.08945; now published in *Science*, doi 10.1126/science.adz9311, per the sibling audit: 29% of US Python functions AI-written by end-2024; +3.6% quarterly output; >30M commits, 170k developers) estimate US value of AI-assisted coding at **$9.6–14.4B/yr** (up to $64–96B under RCT effect sizes; dollar figures are from arXiv v1 and not re-checked in the Science version) — the only independent, non-vendor dollar estimate; Stanford/Denisov-Blanch 15–20% average gain on ~100k developers' private repos (30–40% greenfield, 0–10% brownfield); OpenAI "+70% merged PRs/week"; DORA 2025's positive throughput association (a reversal from 2024). All [P]/[U-consistent] in note S — cite with that caveat.
3. **Add a macro fact from V's own BLS source:** labor share 52.8% in Q2 2026, lowest since 1947. It does not prove AI causation, but it is the aggregate footprint of "gains accrue to capital/vendors, not to workers", which V's value-capture section argues from case evidence.
4. **Software publishers +12.9%:** keep as the strongest industry signal, but add (a) NAICS 5132 includes video-game publishers — so the "software" signal partly *is* the games industry (a useful bridge to the user's question); (b) the industry's output is deflated revenue, and the AI labs' NAICS classification is unknown; (c) the 2024 figure was revised from 9.4% to 4.0%, so 12.9% may move.
5. **Fix the period mismatch** (2026 releases vs 2025 spend) and label Appfigures' AI attribution as a hypothesis.
6. **Vendor revenue confidence:** "High that the revenue is real" → "medium-high" (unaudited run-rates; Cursor's figure spans $2.6–4B depending on outlet; Lovable's $500M is June, not March).
7. **Headcount cases:** Klarna is customer service; Shopify is company-wide; Salesforce's freeze coincided with a guidance miss; Amazon's CEO explicitly said cuts were "not even really AI-driven". Keep them as *illustrations of value captured as margin*, not as evidence of AI-coding value.
8. **J-curve:** V already notes it is unfalsifiable so far; add DORA 2026's explicit J-curve framing and its 15% default dip as the practitioner version of the same idea.

**Recommended balanced formulation (English; the Chinese version follows):**

> As of September 2026, AI coding has produced large, real gains at the task level and a fast-growing tooling industry, but almost no *measurable* incremental value for incumbent software producers or in aggregate productivity statistics. Where value exists it has gone (a) to AI labs and tool vendors as revenue, on thin or negative gross margins; (b) to buyers and users, through IT-services price deflation, subsidized tools and large unpriced consumer surplus; (c) into a long tail of new apps, firms and internal tools whose revenue is small; and (d) into review, rework and organizational absorption costs that consume much of the throughput gain. Output volume (pushes, PRs, apps, share of machine-written code) has grown far faster than any value measure. The honest verdict is therefore: **"volume ≠ value" is true at the incumbent-industry and macro level (high confidence), false for the AI-tool layer (medium-high), and undecidable for social value (large but unpriced).** The J-curve reading ("not yet") and the deflation reading ("captured downstream") both fit the data and cannot yet be told apart; the 2026 BLS industry data (Aug 2027), TFP in 2026–27, SaaS/IT-services revenue re-acceleration, METR's redesigned RCT and the monetization of the 2026 app cohort are the tests.

> 截至 2026 年 9 月：AI 编程在任务层面产生了真实且显著的增益，并催生了一个高速增长的工具产业；但对既有软件生产者的利润和宏观生产率统计而言，几乎看不到可测量的增量价值。已经产生的价值主要流向：(a) 模型厂商与编程工具厂商的收入（毛利率微薄甚至为负）；(b) 采购方与用户（IT 服务"AI 通缩"、补贴定价、未计价的消费者剩余）；(c) 大量长尾的新应用、新公司与内部工具（单体收入很小）；(d) 评审、返工与组织吸收成本（吃掉了相当部分的吞吐量增益）。产出数量（推送、PR、应用数、机器生成代码占比）的增速远高于任何价值口径。因此结论应是：**"量≠价值"在既有产业与宏观层面成立（高置信），在 AI 工具层不成立（中高置信），在社会价值层面无法判定（价值很大但未计价）。**"J 曲线（尚未兑现）"与"价值被下游吃掉"两种解释目前都与数据相容，尚不可分辨。
